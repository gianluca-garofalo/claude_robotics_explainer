SECNUM Worked Example: A Two-Link Manipulator

The hilltop of Section SEC(sec-scalar) is a first-order, one-dimensional problem in which everything can be solved in closed form. This section works through a genuinely robotic case: a two-link arm moving in a vertical plane under gravity, regulated to a fixed posture. The plant is second order, its inertia depends on the configuration, and its torques are bounded. The boundary term is the composite-variable energy of Slotine & Li (1987), the strict alternative to the mechanical energy of Section SEC(sec-strict). The example has three goals: to show what the infinitesimal-window rule produces for this $W$ (a familiar controller), to check the certificate EQ(cert2) honestly, including choices for which it fails, and to run the receding-window scheme and confirm the descent inequality EQ(descent) step by step.

SECNUM The plant

Two rigid, massless links of lengths $l_1=0.5$ m and $l_2=0.4$ m carry point masses $m_1=2.0$ kg and $m_2=1.5$ kg at their tips. Joint angle $q_1$ is measured from the horizontal, $q_2$ is the relative angle of the second link, and gravity $g_0=9.81$ m/s$^2$ acts along $-y$. The equations of motion have the standard form (Spong et al., 2006)

$$ M(q)\,\ddot q+C(q,\dot q)\,\dot q+g(q)=\tau , \tag{EQDEF(armeom)} $$

with $\tau$ the joint torques. Writing $c_2=\cos q_2$, $c_1=\cos q_1$ and $c_{12}=\cos(q_1+q_2)$, the three ingredients are

$$ \begin{aligned} M(q)&=\begin{pmatrix} m_1l_1^2+m_2\big(l_1^2+l_2^2+2l_1l_2c_2\big) & m_2\big(l_2^2+l_1l_2c_2\big)\\[2pt] m_2\big(l_2^2+l_1l_2c_2\big) & m_2l_2^2\end{pmatrix},\\[4pt] C(q,\dot q)&=m_2l_1l_2\,\sin q_2\begin{pmatrix}-\dot q_2 & -(\dot q_1+\dot q_2)\\ \dot q_1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \\[4pt] g(q)&=g_0\begin{pmatrix}(m_1+m_2)\,l_1c_1+m_2l_2c_{12}\\ m_2l_2c_{12}\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \tag{EQDEF(armterms)} $$

$M$ is the mass matrix of the kinetic energy $\mathcal T=\tfrac12v^\top Mv$, $g=\nabla U$ is the gradient of the potential $U=g_0\,(m_1y_1+m_2y_2)$, with $y_1,y_2$ the heights of the two masses, and $C$ is built from the Christoffel symbols of $M$. With that choice of $C$ the matrix $\dot M-2C$ is skew-symmetric, as used in Section SEC(sec-certificate); here

$$ \dot M-2C=m_2l_1l_2\,\sin q_2\,(2\dot q_1+\dot q_2)\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \xi^\top(\dot M-2C)\,\xi=0\ \ \forall \xi . \tag{EQDEF(armskew)} $$

This is the one structural fact the derivation below needs. It says that the Coriolis and centrifugal forces do no work on the kinetic energy (Spong et al., 2006).

The target is the posture $q_d=(\pi/4,\,-\pi/4)$, which places the second link horizontally. We use the error $e=q-q_d$, the velocity $v=\dot q=\dot e$ and the state $z=(e,v)$; the gradient $\nabla_z$ with respect to $(e,v)$ is the same as with respect to $(q,v)$. The input is the gravity-compensated torque

$$ u=\tau-g(q),\qquad\text{so that}\qquad \dot q=v,\qquad \dot v=f(z,u)=M(q)^{-1}\big(u-C(q,v)\,v\big). \tag{EQDEF(armu)} $$

The reason for this change of input is simple: holding the arm still at $q_d$ needs the torque $g(q_d)\ne0$, so a cost on $\tau$ itself would not vanish at the target. In the language of Section SEC(sec-certificate), the cost is centred on the impressed torque $\tau_{\rm imp}=g(q)$. In terms of $u$ the rest point is $z=0$, $u=0$. The torque limits $|\tau_1|\le50$ N m, $|\tau_2|\le30$ N m stay limits on $\tau=u+g(q)$.

At the target, $M(q_d)=\begin{pmatrix}1.539&0.452\\0.452&0.240\end{pmatrix}$ kg m$^2$ with eigenvalues 0.098 and 1.681, a ratio of about 17. The small inertia direction produces a fast closed-loop mode, which matters for the step size in Section SEC(sec-robot-rh).

SECNUM Lagrangian and boundary term

The variational Lagrangian (stage cost) penalizes position error, velocity and the compensated torque:

$$ \ell(z,u)=\tfrac12\,e^\top Q_e\,e+\tfrac12\,v^\top Q_v\,v+\tfrac12\,u^\top R\,u , \qquad Q_e,Q_v,R\succ0 . \tag{EQDEF(armell)} $$

For the boundary term we take the energy-like function of Slotine and Li. It is built from the composite velocity $s$, which blends the velocity with the position error,

$$ s=\dot e+\Gamma e,\qquad W(z)=\tfrac12\,s^\top M(q)\,s+\tfrac12\,e^\top K_e\,e,\qquad \Gamma\succ0,\ K_e\succ0 . \tag{EQDEF(armW)} $$

$W$ is positive definite: it is a kinetic energy evaluated at the “wrong” velocity $s$ plus a spring. To use it as a boundary term we need its rate of change along the plant, as a function of the input. Differentiate, use $M\dot s=M\ddot q+M\Gamma\dot e=u-Cv+M\Gamma\dot e$ from EQ(armu), and replace $\tfrac12s^\top\dot Ms$ by $s^\top Cs$, which is allowed by EQ(armskew). The two Coriolis terms combine, $s^\top C(s-v)=s^\top C\Gamma e$, and what remains is

$$ \dot W(z,u)=s^\top u+s^\top a(z)+e^\top K_e\,\dot e, \qquad a(z)=C(q,v)\,\Gamma e+M(q)\,\Gamma\dot e . \tag{EQDEF(armWdot)} $$

Two features matter. First, $\dot W$ is affine in $u$, and the input enters only through $s^\top u$: the inertia has cancelled. Second, $-a(z)$ is exactly the (gravity-compensated) torque that would be needed to follow the “reference velocity” $\dot q_r=-\Gamma e$, since $M\ddot q_r+C\dot q_r=-a$.

The classical identity. Slotine and Li cancel $a$ and add damping on $s$, $u_{\text{SL}}=-a(z)-K_Ds$, with $K_D\succ0$ and $\Gamma K_D$ symmetric (e.g. both diagonal). Then $\dot W=-s^\top K_Ds+e^\top K_e\dot e$. Expanding $s^\top K_Ds$ produces a cross term $-2e^\top\Gamma K_D\dot e$, which the spring cancels exactly if $K_e=2\Gamma K_D$: $$\dot W=-\dot e^\top K_D\,\dot e-e^\top\Gamma K_D\Gamma\,e\qquad(K_e=2\Gamma K_D,\ u=u_{\text{SL}}).\tag{EQDEF(armSL)}$$ This is the Lyapunov function of the Slotine–Li controller (Slotine & Li, 1987; Slotine & Li, 1991, Ch. 9). It requires the feed-forward $a(z)$, i.e. a model of $M$ and $C$.

SECNUM The infinitesimal-window rule is PD control with gravity compensation

Now apply the rule of Section SEC(sec-so-hj) to this pair $(\ell,W)$: over an infinitesimal window, choose the input that minimizes the action rate plus the rate of change of the boundary term, EQ(rule2). By EQ(armWdot) the bracket is $\ell(z,u)+s^\top u+(\text{terms without }u)$, a strictly convex quadratic in $u$. Setting its gradient $Ru+s$ to zero gives

$$ \begin{gathered} u^*(z)=\arg\min_u\big[\ell(z,u)+\dot W(z,u)\big]=-R^{-1}s,\\ \tau^*=g(q)-K_D\,\dot e-K_P\,e,\quad K_D=R^{-1},\ K_P=R^{-1}\Gamma . \end{gathered} \tag{EQDEF(armrule)} $$
A familiar controller falls out. The infinitesimal window does not produce the Slotine–Li law. It produces PD control with gravity compensation, the controller of Takegaki & Arimoto (1981), with damping gain $K_D=R^{-1}$ and stiffness $K_P=R^{-1}\Gamma$ (symmetric when $R$ and $\Gamma$ commute, e.g. both diagonal, as here). Three observations explain why.
  • The input enters $\dot W$ only through $s^\top u$, so the minimizer sees only $s$. The feed-forward $a(z)$ does not depend on $u$ and therefore cannot lower $\ell+\dot W$; the rule has no reason to cancel it.
  • In momentum language, the velocity component of $\pi=-\nabla_zW$ is $\pi_v=-M(q)\,s$, and the input acts through $M^{-1}$. The maximizer of $\pi_v\cdot M^{-1}u-\tfrac12u^\top Ru$ is $R^{-1}M^{-1}\pi_v=-R^{-1}s$, as in EQ(manip): the configuration-dependent inertia cancels, which is why constant gains come out.
  • Compare Section SEC(sec-certificate): with the mechanical energy the velocity momentum was $-M\dot q$, and the same rule produced only damping. The composite velocity puts the position error into the velocity momentum, $\pi_v=-M(\dot e+\Gamma e)$, and the rule now produces stiffness $K_P=R^{-1}\Gamma$ as well. This is the weak/strict distinction of Section SEC(sec-strict) seen from the controller side.
The only model knowledge left is $g(q)$, which was put in by the choice $u=\tau-g$. The price is paid in the certificate: $a(z)$ stays in $\dot W$, and Section SEC(sec-robot-condition) shows that the gains must dominate it.

SECNUM Checking the certificate

Whether $W$ certifies descent is decided by the value of the bracket at its minimizer, the descent defect of Section SEC(sec-so-hj):

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal D(z)&=\min_u\big[\ell(z,u)+\dot W(z,u)\big]\\ &=\tfrac12e^\top Q_ee+\tfrac12v^\top Q_vv-\tfrac12s^\top R^{-1}s+s^\top a(z)+e^\top K_e\dot e\\ &=-H\big(z,-\nabla_zW\big), \end{aligned} \tag{EQDEF(armD)} $$

where $H(z,\pi)=\sup_u[\pi_q\cdot v+\pi_v\cdot f(z,u)-\ell]$ is the second-order Hamiltonian EQ(H2). The last equality holds because, for $\pi=-\nabla_zW$, $\pi_q\cdot v+\pi_v\cdot f(z,u)=-\nabla_zW\cdot\dot z=-\dot W(z,u)$. Along $u^*$ we have $\dot W=-\ell+\mathcal D$, so the requirement $\dot W\le-\ell$ is $\mathcal D(z)\le0$, i.e. $H(z,-\nabla_zW)\ge0$: the certificate EQ(cert2). It has to hold on a terminal region $\Omega$, here a sublevel set $\{W\le \bar W\}$.

Near the target: matching the Lagrangian to $W$

Close to $z=0$ the sign of $\mathcal D$ is decided by its quadratic part. The Coriolis part of $a(z)$ is quadratic in $z$, so it contributes only cubic terms. Freezing $M$ at $M_0=M(q_d)$ in the other part gives $\mathcal D(z)=\tfrac12z^\top\nabla^2\mathcal D(0)\,z+O(|z|^3)$ with

$$ \nabla^2\mathcal D(0)=\begin{pmatrix} Q_e-\Gamma K_D\Gamma & K_e-\Gamma K_D+\Gamma M_0\Gamma\\[2pt] \big(K_e-\Gamma K_D+\Gamma M_0\Gamma\big)^\top & Q_v-K_D+M_0\Gamma+\Gamma M_0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad K_D=R^{-1}, \tag{EQDEF(armhess)} $$

for diagonal $\Gamma$ and $R$. The off-diagonal block of EQ(armhess) is the coupling between position error and velocity. It vanishes for one spring only, and then the diagonal blocks give explicit bounds on the Lagrangian:

$$ K_e=\Gamma K_D-\Gamma M_0\Gamma,\qquad Q_e\preceq\Gamma K_D\Gamma,\qquad Q_v\preceq K_D-(M_0\Gamma+\Gamma M_0). \tag{EQDEF(armmatch)} $$

With strict inequalities, $\mathcal D\le-\alpha_0|z|^2$ near the target for some $\alpha_0\gt0$. With equalities, $\nabla^2\mathcal D(0)=0$ and $W$ matches $\ell$ to second order, the analogue of $H=0$; the cubic terms then decide, and they are odd in $z$. With $\Gamma=\gamma I$, a positive-definite spring needs $K_D\succ\gamma M_0$, and a positive-definite $Q_v$ needs $K_D\succ2\gamma M_0$: the damping must dominate the inertia seen through $\Gamma$.

The Slotine–Li spring is the wrong spring here. Give the Slotine–Li law the same damping as the rule, $K_D=R^{-1}$ as in EQ(armrule); its matching spring EQ(armSL) is then $K_e=2\Gamma R^{-1}$. With $\Gamma=\gamma I$ and $K_e=2\gamma K_D$, the off-diagonal block is $\gamma K_D+\gamma^2M_0$. For $Q_e=Q_v=0$ the Schur complement of the (negative-definite) upper-left block of $\nabla^2\mathcal D(0)$ is $4\gamma M_0+\gamma^2M_0K_D^{-1}M_0\succ0$, so $\nabla^2\mathcal D(0)$ has positive eigenvalues; adding $Q_e,Q_v\succeq0$ only raises them. Hence no stage cost of the form EQ(armell) is certified by the Slotine–Li gain matching. The reason is a factor of two: $\min_u[\tfrac12u^\top Ru+s^\top u]=-\tfrac12s^\top R^{-1}s$ supplies only half of the damping $s^\top K_Ds$ that EQ(armSL) uses, and $u^*$ does not cancel $a(z)$. This is a concrete instance of the caution of Section SEC(sec-plant-energy): the Lagrangian must match $W$. The closed loop is still stable (for set-point regulation, PD with exact gravity compensation and without torque limits is globally asymptotically stable for any symmetric positive-definite gains; Takegaki & Arimoto, 1981); what fails is the certificate, exactly as in Case B of Section SEC(sec-numeric).

Chosen parameters

We take $\Gamma=\gamma I$ with $\gamma=2$ s$^{-1}$ and $R=\operatorname{diag}(0.05,\,0.1)$, so $K_D=\operatorname{diag}(20,\,10)$ and $K_P=\operatorname{diag}(40,\,20)$. The matched spring EQ(armmatch) is $K_e=\begin{pmatrix}33.84&-1.81\\-1.81&19.04\end{pmatrix}$, positive definite. The bounds in EQ(armmatch) are $\Gamma K_D\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(80,40)$ and $K_D-2\gamma M_0$ with eigenvalues 8.44 and 14.45; we use half the first, $Q_e=\operatorname{diag}(40,\,20)$, and a round value below half the second, $Q_v=4I$ (the smallest eigenvalue of $\tfrac12(K_D-2\gamma M_0)$ is 4.22, so $\tfrac12(K_D-2\gamma M_0)-4I\succ0$).

How far does the certificate hold?

The largest admissible level is $\bar W^*=\inf\{W(z):\mathcal D(z)\gt0\}$. We estimated it numerically: along $2\times10^5$ random rays in the four-dimensional state space we located the first point with $\mathcal D\gt0$ (out to $|z|=30$) by bisection, took the smallest $W$ found, and refined it by a local minimization of $W$ subject to $\mathcal D\ge0$. Three readings. The chosen design satisfies the certificate on a set far larger than any motion of interest (the failure point found lies at about 10 rad of joint error and 17 rad/s), so for this arm the Hamilton–Jacobi inequality is not the binding restriction on $\Omega$; the torque limits are (Section SEC(sec-robot-rh)). The $M_0$ correction to the spring is not essential once $Q_e$, $Q_v$ have a margin; the factor of two is. And a Lagrangian that matches $W$ exactly to second order is as fragile here as the continuous-time Riccati weight was in Section SEC(sec-numeric): a margin is what makes the certificate robust.

SECNUM The receding-window scheme

A digital controller needs a step map. We use the semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler step with $h=0.005$ s,

$$ \begin{gathered} v_{k+1}=v_k+h\,M(q_k)^{-1}\big(u_k-C(q_k,v_k)v_k\big),\qquad e_{k+1}=e_k+h\,v_{k+1},\\ z_{k+1}=\Phi_h(z_k,u_k), \end{gathered} \tag{EQDEF(armstep)} $$

the rectangle-rule stage cost $h\,\ell(z_k,u_k)$ and the terminal cost $c\,W$ on the terminal region $\Omega=\{W\le \bar W\}$, with a scale $c$ as in Section SEC(sec-exactness). The reference flow of Section SEC(sec-terminal) is the PD law EQ(armrule) itself, $u_{\text{ref}}=-R^{-1}s$, and the discrete condition EQ(terminal) reads

$$ c\,\big[W(\Phi_h(z,u_{\text{ref}}))-W(z)\big]\ \le\ -h\,\ell(z,u_{\text{ref}})\qquad\forall z\in\Omega, \tag{EQDEF(armterminal)} $$

together with the torque limits for $\tau=g(q)+u_{\text{ref}}$ on $\Omega$. Since $W$ then decreases under $u_{\text{ref}}$, the sublevel set $\Omega$ is invariant (A1). Three numbers fix the terminal ingredients.

  • Torque limits. The reference torque stays inside the limits on $\{W\le \bar W\}$ for $\bar W\le\bar W_\tau\approx0.310$. The first violation is $\tau_2=30$ N m, almost at the target posture, $e\approx(0.0006,\,0.013)$ rad, at a fast joint-2 velocity, $v\approx(0.71,\,-2.44)$ rad/s. At $e=0$ the bound can be read off: there $s=v$, and EQ(armrule) gives $\tau_2=g_2(q_d)-(K_D)_{22}v_2$ with $g_2(q_d)=g_0m_2l_2=5.89$ N m, so the limit is reached at $v_2=-(30-g_2(q_d))/(K_D)_{22}=-2.41$ rad/s. The least $W=\tfrac12v^\top M_0v$ with this $v_2$, attained at $v_1=-(M_0)_{12}v_2/(M_0)_{11}=0.71$ rad/s, is $\tfrac12\big[(M_0)_{22}-(M_0)_{12}^2/(M_0)_{11}\big]v_2^2=0.312$, an upper bound on $\bar W_\tau$. The level itself is the least $W$ on the faces $\tau_i=\pm\tau_{\max,i}$ of the box. On such a face EQ(armrule) fixes $s_i=\big(g_i(q)\mp\tau_{\max,i}\big)/(K_D)_{ii}$ as a function of $e$, while the other component $s_j$ is left free (this minimizes over the plane of the face, a lower bound for the face itself); minimizing EQ(armW) over $s_j$ leaves $\tfrac12\big(M_{ii}-M_{ij}^2/M_{jj}\big)(q)\,s_i^2+\tfrac12e^\top K_ee$, a function of $e$ alone, and since $W\ge\tfrac12e^\top K_ee\ge9.4\,|e|^2$ it needs to be minimized only over a bounded disk of $e$. This gives 0.310 on the face $\tau_2=+30$ N m; the other three faces are reached only at $W\ge0.68$. We take $\bar W=0.3$, about 3% below $\bar W_\tau$.
  • Discretization. The smallest admissible scale is $c\ge\sup_\Omega h\ell/(W-W\circ \Phi_h)$. Near the target this is a generalized eigenvalue problem of the linearization; on $\Omega$ we sampled $4\times10^5$ points and refined the worst ones. For $h=0.005$ s the supremum is 0.965 near the target and 0.994 on $\Omega$, so $c=1$ suffices: the unscaled $W$ is a valid terminal cost.
  • Why not $h=0.01$ s? Then the required scale is 1.69 near the target and 2.27 on $\Omega$. The culprit is the fast mode of the closed loop (eigenvalue $\sigma=-109.5$ s$^{-1}$, so $h|\sigma|\approx1.1$), for which the rectangle rule is a poor approximation of the action. Inflating $W$ would work, as in Section SEC(sec-exactness), but to first order in $h$ it also multiplies the gain of the one-step rule by $c$ (the rule for $cW$ is $u=-cR^{-1}s$), so we halve the step instead.
At $h=0.005$ s the margin is small ($c=1$ against a sampled requirement of 0.994). It exists only because the weights leave slack in EQ(armmatch): $Q_e$ sits at half its bound and $Q_v=4I$ below half of its bound. The slack is barely enough: with $Q_v$ at exactly half its bound, $\tfrac12(K_D-2\gamma M_0)$, the requirement near the target would already be 1.035, and $c=1$ would fail. A design at the knife edge would violate EQ(armterminal) with $c=1$ for small $h$, because its continuous-time limit $\mathcal D\le0$ already fails.
One cycle of the scheme for the arm
  1. Measure $z_k=(q_k-q_d,\dot q_k)$.
  2. Solve, over planned torques $\tau_{0|k},\dots,\tau_{N-1|k}$, each inside the box $|\tau_1|\le50$, $|\tau_2|\le30$ N m, $$V_N(z_k)=\min\ \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}h\,\ell\big(z_{j|k},\tau_{j|k}-g(q_{j|k})\big)+W(z_{N|k}),$$ subject to the step map EQ(armstep), $z_{0|k}=z_k$ and the terminal constraint $W(z_{N|k})\le \bar W$; the subscript $j|k$ marks node $j$ of the plan computed at step $k$. Single shooting, SciPy SLSQP, exact gradients by the adjoint (backward) recursion.
  3. Warm start: the previous solution shifted by one step, with one reference step $u_{\text{ref}}$ appended at the predicted terminal state. This is the feasible candidate of the proof in Section SEC(sec-descent). If the solver returns a worse cost than the candidate, keep the candidate.
  4. Apply $\tau_{0|k}$ for one step and repeat.

The table summarizes 2.5 s (500 steps). $S_{\text{cl}}=\sum_kh\ell(z_k,u_k)$ is the action actually paid. Write $\ell_k=\ell(z_k,u_k)$. The ratio column reports the smallest value over all steps of the descent ratio $[V_N(z_k)-V_N(z_{k+1})]/h\ell_k$. By EQ(armell), $\ell_k\gt0$ whenever $z_k\ne0$, so EQ(descent), which here reads $V_N(z_{k+1})-V_N(z_k)\le-h\ell_k$, holds at step $k$ exactly when this ratio is at least 1, and its excess over 1 is the margin of EQ(descent) relative to the action spent: a ratio of 1.09 means that the remaining action fell by 9% more than the action spent. The difference $V_N(z_{k+1})-V_N(z_k)+h\ell_k$ itself is no measure of this margin: it equals $-h\ell_k$ times the excess, and $h\ell_k$ shrinks with the state, so in every run its largest value comes in the last steps, where $V_N\lt10^{-6}$, and only records how small the state is after 2.5 s.

  • The certificate does not depend on $N$. In all six receding-window runs the descent ratio never drops below 1.09, so $V_N(z_{k+1})-V_N(z_k)+h\ell_k\le-0.09\,h\ell_k\lt0$ at every step. Since the solver keeps the shifted candidate whenever it does worse (Section SEC(sec-robot-rh)), this descent follows from EQ(armterminal) whatever the solver’s optimality, as in suboptimal MPC: the runs confirm that EQ(armterminal) held along the path, not that each computed $V_N$ is the exact minimum.
  • Short windows are aggressive. From the first start, the $N=10$ window must bring $W$ from 1.190 down to 0.3 within 50 ms. The terminal constraint is active for the first 9 steps, both torques run into their limits (50 and 30 N m), and the action paid is 2.463, 4.8 times that of PD$+g$. With $N=20$ the constraint is active for 14 steps and the action drops to 0.799; with $N=40$ to 0.541 (4 active steps). Both windows close the slow joint-1 error faster than PD$+g$, because the spring $K_e$ in $W$ prices position error more heavily than $\ell$ does.
  • A longer window lowers $V_N$, not necessarily the action paid. $V_N(z_0)$ decreases with $N$ toward the long-window value, as EQ(monotone) predicts. The action actually paid in closed loop need not decrease, and for the windows simulated it stays above that of PD$+g$, which is within 2.3% (first start) and 2.4% (second start) of the best long-window cost found (a local optimum, warm-started from the PD path). The reason is the margin built into $W$: with $Q_e$ at half its bound and $Q_v=4I$ below half of its bound in EQ(armmatch), $W$ over-estimates the remaining action by a factor of about 2.3 at the second start, and a short window optimizes against that over-estimate. This is the price of a robust certificate.
  • One step is the discrete PD law. The closed-loop action of the $N=1$ run is within 0.5% of that of PD$+g$. At the states it visits, its torque differs from the PD$+g$ torque EQ(armrule) by at most 0.73 and 1.03 N m (joints 1 and 2). The gap builds up over the first 40–50 ms, while the fast mode decays, and then shrinks with the state. These are the $O(h)$ terms that separate the one-step window from the infinitesimal one: at the state of the largest gap, re-solving the one-step problem with $h/2$, $h/4$, $h/8$ shrinks the joint-2 gap to 0.63, 0.35, 0.19 N m: each halving of $h$ cuts it by a factor that approaches one half, as expected for an $O(h)$ gap.

SECNUM Torque limits and the terminal constraint as inequalities

The arm’s torque limits and its terminal region are inequalities, and every number above respects them. The theory of Sections SEC(sec-kkt), SEC(sec-constrained-v) and SEC(sec-so-discrete) says three things about them.

  • The infinitesimal rule with the torque box is saturated PD control. The box is a constraint $u\in\mathcal U(z)$ on the gravity-compensated input, and by EQ(ruleV), in its phase-space form, the rule minimizes the quadratic $\ell+\dot W$ over it. For the diagonal $R$ used here the minimizer is the componentwise clip of EQ(armrule), $$\tau^*=\mathrm{clip}\big(g(q)-K_D\dot e-K_Pe,\ -\tau_{\max},\ \tau_{\max}\big),\tag{EQDEF(armclip)}$$ which is how the PD$+g$ runs of Section SEC(sec-robot-results) were implemented (their torques never reached the limits). A QP solver confirms the clip on 2000 random states, 1515 of them saturated, to $3\times10^{-14}$. With Gauss’s weight $R=T_0M^{-1}$ the minimizer is instead the $M^{-1}$-metric projection of the unconstrained torque, which is not a clip: at the most extreme of the same states ($T_0=0.05$ s) the unconstrained torque $(214.1,\,37.3)$ N m projects to $(50.0,\,-10.2)$ N m, whereas clipping gives $(50.0,\,30.0)$ N m. Saturating the shoulder reverses the best elbow torque, because the kinetic metric couples the joints.
  • The certificate survives saturation far beyond the torque-limit level. Since the minimum over the box is no smaller than the unconstrained one, $\mathcal D_{\mathcal U}(z)=\min_{u\in\mathcal U(z)}[\ell+\dot W]\ge\mathcal D(z)$. The level $\bar W_\tau\approx0.310$ of Section SEC(sec-robot-rh) is the level below which the box never binds for the reference law, so there $\mathcal D_{\mathcal U}=\mathcal D$. Beyond it, the saturated law still certifies descent in continuous time on $\{W\le\bar W\}$ for $\bar W$ up to about 5.9 (sampled ray search with local refinement, as in Section SEC(sec-robot-condition)), against about $10^3$ without the box. In discrete time ($h=0.005$ s, saturated reference law, which is admissible by construction) the smallest admissible scale in EQ(armterminal), $\sup h\ell/(W-W\circ\Phi_h)$ over the set, is 1.048 on $\{W\le1\}$ and 1.137 on $\{W\le4\}$, so $c=1.05$ and $c=1.14$ suffice (sampled, with the worst samples refined by local maximization as in Section SEC(sec-robot-rh)). In both sets the worst state lies on the boundary $W=\bar W$, exactly where the unclipped $\tau_2$ in EQ(armclip) reaches its 30 N m limit. Letting the reference law saturate would enlarge the terminal region more than tenfold, at the price of a slightly inflated $W$.
  • The active terminal constraint inflates the boundary term. At the first step of the $N=10$ run from $(1.0,-0.95)$, six of the twenty planned torques sit at a bound, all with multipliers of the correct sign, and the terminal constraint $W(z_N)\le0.3$ is active with $\mu=39.2$. By EQ(msineq) the window plans as if its boundary term were $(1+\mu)W\approx40\,W$, which is why its first move is so aggressive (Section SEC(sec-robot-results)). Along the run $\mu$ falls to 25.9, 17.8, 12.4, 8.4, 5.5, 3.3, 1.7 and 0.59, and the constraint releases at the tenth step, in agreement with the nine active steps reported there.

SECNUM Summary

What the arm teaches.
  1. For the composite energy $W=\tfrac12s^\top Ms+\tfrac12e^\top K_ee$ and a quadratic Lagrangian in the gravity-compensated input, the infinitesimal-window rule is PD control with gravity compensation, $K_D=R^{-1}$, $K_P=R^{-1}\Gamma$ EQ(armrule). The inertia cancels; the feed-forward of Slotine and Li is not selected.
  2. The certificate is $\mathcal D=-H(z,-\nabla_zW)\le0$. Near the target it reduces to the explicit matching conditions EQ(armmatch). The Slotine–Li spring $K_e=2\Gamma K_D$, correct for its own controller, violates them for every admissible $Q_e$, $Q_v$: a good argmin with a failed certificate.
  3. For this arm the binding limits on the terminal region are the actuators and the step size, not the Hamilton–Jacobi inequality. With $h=0.005$ s the plain $W$ is a valid discrete terminal cost; with $h=0.01$ s it must be inflated by more than 2.
  4. The window length behaves as Section SEC(sec-onestep) predicts: it does not change the certificate, it enlarges the solvable set (${N=1}$ and ${N=5}$ cannot start where ${N=10}$ can) and it lowers $V_N$. It does not automatically lower the action paid: with a conservative $W$ and a hard terminal constraint, short windows are more aggressive than the PD law they generalize.
  5. Torque limits and the terminal region are inequalities. With the torque box the infinitesimal rule is saturated PD$+g$ EQ(armclip), the certificate survives saturation on a set far larger than the torque-limit level, and an active terminal constraint plans with the inflated boundary term $(1+\mu)W$ ($\mu=39.2$ at the first step of the $N=10$ run).
