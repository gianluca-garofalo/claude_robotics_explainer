SECNUM Power, Ports and Energy Tanks

Sections SEC(sec-hierarchy) and SEC(sec-ineq-hier) attributed forces to tasks in a basis that depends on the priority order and on the active set. This section asks which power flows survive that choice, and what this means for energy tanks.

SECNUM Total power is invariant

Combine EQ(Jbar) and EQ(forcebasis): the power injected through the task channel is

$$ \tau_x^\top\dot q=F^\top \zeta=\lambda^\top\dot x , \tag{EQDEF(power)} $$

because $\tau_x^\top\dot q=F^\top\bar J\dot q=F^\top\zeta$ and $F^\top \zeta=\lambda^\top\Theta\,\zeta=\lambda^\top\dot x$. This is the pairing of a covector with a velocity (Section SEC(sec-covectors)): the total is the same in every basis and for every priority order. Its split into levels, $F_i^\top \zeta_i$, is not. In the example of Section SEC(sec-forces) the total is $-0.391$ in both orders, while the per-level powers are $(3.057,\,-3.416,\,-0.032)$ for tasks $(1,2,3)$ in the order $1\succ2\succ3$, and $(0.033,\,-0.446,\,0.022)$ for the same tasks in the reversed order.

SECNUM Power with unilateral forces and at switches

Three statements, each checked on the examples of Sections SEC(sec-unilateral) and SEC(sec-ineq-switch):

  • Totals are continuous, attributions are not. In the constraint view, for a feasible stack, the torque, and with it the total power EQ(power), is continuous across activation and deactivation. The levels above the switching level keep their power; the switching level and the levels below exchange it by re-attribution ($-89.066$ on the last level becomes $-13.476-75.590$ in the example).
  • The power of a unilateral force has no sign. It vanishes on a persistent contact, and it is positive for an active, feedback-bounded inequality task whose coordinate recedes ($+0.805$ in the example).
  • In the impressed view the total jumps as well, because the torque does: it changes by $\Delta\tau_x^\top\dot q$, nonzero in general, unless the prescribed forces are re-mapped through EQ(forcebasis) at the switch (at a deactivation this also needs the stacked multiplier of the leaving row to be zero, Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch)). In the first example of Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch), with $F_1$ and $F_3$ held and $F_2=0$ at the activation, the level powers are $-10.024$, $0$ and $-75.590$: the total $-99.090$ becomes $-10.024-75.590$, a jump of $+13.476$, which is minus the power $-13.476$ of the re-mapped $F_2=-11.501$.

SECNUM Consequences for tanks

Energy tanks make a controller passive by routing every non-passive power flow through a storage element that must stay positive (Dietrich et al., 2017; Garofalo & Ott, 2018). Sections SEC(sec-invariance) and SEC(sec-ineq-power) say what such a tank observes:

  • a single tank on the total task port sees $\tau_x^\top\dot q=\lambda^\top\dot x=F^\top \zeta$ EQ(power), which for a given task torque $\tau_x$ does not depend on the priority order used to attribute it. In the constraint view with a feasible stack the torque itself is the same for every order (Section SEC(sec-lexi)) and continuous across active-set switches (Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch)), and so is this flow. In the impressed view, with prescribed $F_i$, the torque $\bar J^\top F$, and with it the flow, in general changes with the order and jumps at a switch, because $\bar J$ does, unless the forces are re-mapped through EQ(forcebasis) (at a deactivation only if the stacked multiplier of the leaving row is zero, Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch));
  • per-level tanks see $F_i^\top \zeta_i$: flows that depend on the order and jump when the active set changes, even when the torque does not. A level can appear with a nonzero decoupled force and zero multiplier ($F_2=-11.50$ in Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch)), and an inequality task’s own power can be positive.

Chattering. In discrete time an active set can toggle from one sample to the next near a bound. Every toggle re-attributes power at and below the switching level even when the torque barely changes, so per-level tanks would see spurious exchanges, and a single tank on the total port would not. Hysteresis in the activation, or a bound placed at the velocity or position level instead of the acceleration level, are natural remedies. Tanks for strict hierarchies exist (Dietrich et al., 2017), but we know of no analysis of tank bookkeeping across inequality active-set switches.

Design question this raises. If per-level tanks are used (to make each level individually passive), the bookkeeping at switches must transfer energy between levels consistently with the change of basis EQ(forcebasis): at an activation or deactivation at level $i$ only level $i$ and the levels below are affected, and the transfer must also absorb the power of unilateral forces, which has no sign. How to do that while preserving strict priority, and without chattering, is, to our knowledge, not settled in general; it is the most concrete open problem suggested by this viewpoint.

SECNUM Windows, Priorities and Whole-Body MPC

Part I shrank a receding window to an instant; the preceding sections split one instant among priority levels. This section puts the two side by side.

SECNUM Is whole-body control the zero-window limit of whole-body MPC?

Parts I and II give a qualified answer.

  • With an energy-type boundary term, only if the stiffness is put in by hand. The torque enters the $\dot W$ of EQ(energyW) only through the power $\dot q^\top\tau$, so the infinitesimal rule EQ(centred) adds pure damping, $-R^{-1}\dot q$, to the torque on which the stage cost is centred (Sections SEC(sec-certificate)–SEC(sec-strict)). A task-error penalty in $\ell_0$ does not depend on $\tau$, so it drops out of the argmin. The stiffness it gives a whole-body MPC over a finite window is therefore lost in the zero-window limit. The penalty then enters only the certificate, which it violates at rest away from the target (Section SEC(sec-strict)). Centred on $\tau_{\rm imp}=g-\nabla U_s$, with $U_s$ the potential in $W$, the rule is the passivity-based law with natural inertia of Section SEC(sec-certificate). Its stiffness comes from the centring, not from the minimization, and the energy certifies only the damping EQ(defectE). Under strict priority the centring torque is in general not of this form, and no single $W$ of the form EQ(energyW) certifies the result (Sections SEC(sec-leak) and SEC(sec-cascade)). The minimization itself produces stiffness only with a $W$ that has position–velocity cross terms (Section SEC(sec-robot)), or with a finite window. In the latter case the resulting gains depend on how the torque is held over the window (zero-order hold, impulse, or time-varying), not only on the window length. Take the one-degree-of-freedom setup of Section SEC(sec-gauss-free): mass $M=2$ kg, $W=\tfrac12Mv^2+\tfrac12K_eq^2$ with $K_e=30$ N/m, Gauss’s weight with $T_0=0.1$ s centred on zero torque ($\tau_{\rm imp}=0$, so the spring sits only in $W$ and the infinitesimal rule $\tau=-Mv/T_0$ has no stiffness), no other stage cost, window $\vartheta=10$ ms. With the torque held constant over the window, the window problem is a scalar quadratic in the torque, and its minimizer has the first-instant stiffness $K_e\vartheta/{\big[2\big(T_0+\vartheta+K_e\vartheta^3/(4M)\big)\big]}=1.36$ N/m. With the optimal time-varying torque, the window is solved by the Riccati differential equation that EQ(hj) becomes in phase space for a quadratic boundary term (as in Section SEC(sec-scalar)), started at $P_0=\mathrm{diag}(K_e,M)$, and the first-instant stiffness is $(P_\vartheta)_{12}/T_0=2.86$ N/m.
  • With the value function of a longer horizon as $W$, yes in spirit. A one-step rule whose boundary term is the value function of a longer-horizon problem is the construction behind value-function-based whole-body control in cascaded MPC (Li & Wensing, 2025). With a control Lyapunov function in that role, the one-step rule is a pointwise controller built on that function, the kind of law that Primbs et al. (2000) generalize to receding horizons (Section SEC(sec-allwindows)). In continuous time it is the infinitesimal-window rule EQ(rule), the zero-window end of the receding window that keeps the function as boundary term (Section SEC(sec-infinitesimal)), the terminal-cost scheme of Chen & Allgöwer (1998) and Jadbabaie et al. (2001). Primbs et al. generalize by a different construction. In their window problem the control Lyapunov function enters as constraints, not as a boundary term: a decrease condition on the first move, and an end-of-window condition that its value at the end of the window not exceed its value at the end of the path the pointwise min-norm controller would follow over the same window. As the window shrinks to zero their scheme reduces to pointwise min-norm control, which prescribes a rate at which $W$ must decrease and takes the least-norm velocity that achieves it; under further conditions it approaches optimal control as the window grows without bound. The rule EQ(rule) is of that kind, with $L$ in place of the norm and with the rate it delivers itself: for every $v\in\mathcal V(q)$ with $\nabla W\cdot v\le\nabla W\cdot v^*$, minimality of $v^*$ gives $L(q,v)\ge L(q,v^*)+\nabla W\cdot(v^*-v)\ge L(q,v^*)$. The two zero-window ends therefore agree where the prescribed rate equals $-\nabla W\cdot v^*$, and need not agree elsewhere.
  • Multipliers. In a multiple-shooting transcription the defect multipliers are discrete momenta (Section SEC(sec-so-discrete)), and the first torque equals the first velocity momentum over $T_0$: exactly in continuous time; in discrete time, exactly under symplectic Euler when neither $b$ nor the stage cost depends on $v$, $T_0\tau_0=\pi_{v,0}=-\nabla_vV_N(z_0)$ with $\pi_0$ the multiplier of the initial value; as the mean $T_0\tau_0=\tfrac12(\pi_{v,0}+\pi_{v,1})$ under zero-order hold with constant $M$ and $b$; and to $O(h)$ otherwise (Section SEC(sec-gauss-free)). The mean is why the held-torque stiffness above is about half the time-varying one. Per unit initial displacement, the velocity momentum of the held window gives $-\pi_{v,0}/T_0=2.864$ N/m at its start, essentially the time-varying first-instant value 2.86 N/m, and $-\pi_{v,1}/T_0=-0.136$ N/m at its end; the held torque takes their mean, 1.36 N/m.

SECNUM Invariant versus attributed: windows and priorities

Two design choices in this document leave a certificate-level quantity untouched and redistribute something else. The parallel is structural, not a theorem; this subsection states what is exact and where it stops.

Window length. With an exact pair, $W=V_\infty$ for the discrete problem and no active constraint, the fixed-point property of Section SEC(sec-exactness) gives $V_N=V_\infty$ for every $N$: the window length only moves the cut between the computed part of the remaining action (the window) and the estimated part (the tail $W$). With an inexact pair that passes the one-step test EQ(terminal) the total itself changes with $N$, downward by EQ(monotone), while the certificate $(W,\Omega)$ stays the same; a pair that fails EQ(terminal) has no certificate to keep, and its total can rise with $N$ instead (the textbook weight of Section SEC(sec-split)). The table of Section SEC(sec-split) shows both: $V_N(q_0)=0.5000$ for every $N$ with the exact pair, against $0.5857$ at $N=1$ falling to $0.5269$ at $N=20$ (towards $P_d/2=0.5256$) with the inflated rectangle-rule pair.

Priority order. For a given task torque $\tau_x$ (in either view), every priority order gives the same total power EQ(power), and only its split among levels changes (Section SEC(sec-invariance)). In the constraint view with a feasible stack the torque itself does not depend on the order, with or without active inequality rows: the stack is then a single QP (Section SEC(sec-lexi)), whose solution, and with it the active set, is the same for every order. The order is then a pure change of basis, and an active-set switch only re-attributes forces and power at and below the switching level, with the torque continuous (Sections SEC(sec-ineq-switch) and SEC(sec-ineq-power)). Under a conflict, that is, when the tasks cannot all be met at once (incompatible targets at a singularity of the stack, Section SEC(sec-consistency), or an inequality bound that cannot be held together with the other tasks, Section SEC(sec-soft)), or in the impressed view where each level’s force is prescribed, the order can change the torque and the motion.

The parallel stops at the level of structure. The window length moves a cut in time, the priority order a cut among task coordinates, and nothing in the window problem plays the role of the non-integrable projections that prevent a single energy certificate for a strict hierarchy (Section SEC(sec-leak)).

SECNUM Toward hierarchical certificates

For hierarchical natural-inertia controllers the value-function construction of Section SEC(sec-mpc-limit) meets the obstacle of Section SEC(sec-leak): there is no $W$ of the form EQ(energyW) for a strict hierarchy, and no general construction of a strict one is known to us. A hierarchical receding-horizon certificate would therefore most naturally be cascaded level by level: a boundary term per level in the decoupled coordinates $\zeta_i$, with the Coriolis couplings $\bar C_{ij}$ of Section SEC(sec-consistency) treated as interconnection terms, as the level-by-level stability analyses cited in Section SEC(sec-leak) do. Whether per-level descent defects can be made to add up, and how the per-level power of Section SEC(sec-power) enters them at active-set switches, is open.

SECNUM Floating Base and Contacts (Outlook)

For a floating-base robot the reading needs one more constraint. Six coordinates give the pose of the base; write $(\cdot)_{\rm base}$ for the six base rows of a vector and $M_{\rm base}$ for those of $M$. With $J_c$ the Jacobian of the closed contacts and $f_c$ their wrenches, the equations of motion are $M\ddot q+b=\tau+J_c^\top f_c$, and the base is unactuated, $\tau_{\rm base}=0$. The impressed torque must then be a joint torque too, $(\tau_{\rm imp})_{\rm base}=0$: gravity can be compensated in the joint rows only, and the weight has to be carried by $f_c$. The base rows $$\big(M\ddot q+b-J_c^\top f_c\big)_{\rm base}=0$$ are the Newton–Euler equations of the whole robot. In inertially decoupled coordinates that separate the centroidal dynamics from the joint dynamics (Garofalo et al., 2015) they say that only gravity and the contact wrenches change the centroidal momentum, which is the statement behind momentum-based whole-body control (in the decoupled velocity coordinates the angular part still keeps Coriolis coupling with the joints).

The Gauss problems of Sections SEC(sec-gauss-task)–SEC(sec-unilateral) do not contain these rows. With the contacts as rows (Section SEC(sec-gauss-dual), multipliers $\lambda_c$) the correction is $\tau_c=J^\top\lambda+J_c^\top\lambda_c$. Read with $f_c=\lambda_c$, it asks the actuators for the base rows $(J^\top\lambda)_{\rm base}$, which do not vanish in general: a task expressed in the world frame, such as a hand position, depends on the pose of the base. The base rows therefore enter Gauss’s problem as constraints, with $f_c$ as an additional unknown and $\lambda_{\rm base}$ as their multiplier. Stationarity in $\ddot q$ gives $M(\ddot q-\ddot q_{\rm free})=J^\top\lambda+J_c^\top\lambda_c+M_{\rm base}^\top\lambda_{\rm base}$, and stationarity in $f_c$ makes $\lambda_{\rm base}$ orthogonal to every base wrench the contacts can exert, $\lambda_{\rm base}^\top(J_c^\top f)_{\rm base}=0$ for all $f$. Two cases follow.

  • The contacts can exert any base wrench (as equalities: one foot or both feet flat on the ground, or three point contacts not on one line). Then $\lambda_{\rm base}=0$, and the motion and the multipliers are those of the stack without the base rows. Their reading changes. The contact wrench is $f_c=\lambda_c+\Delta f_c$, with $(J_c^\top\Delta f_c)_{\rm base}=(J^\top\lambda)_{\rm base}$ and $\Delta f_c$ unique up to internal forces, and the actuators add $J^\top\lambda-J_c^\top\Delta f_c$ to $\tau_{\rm imp}$, not $J^\top\lambda$. On persistent contacts ($J_c\dot q=0$) the difference does no work, so EQ(power) still gives the power that the actuators inject on top of $\tau_{\rm imp}$.
  • They cannot (one or two point contacts, a line contact, flight). Then the base rows bind, in general, in the directions of base wrench that the contacts cannot exert, and the task multipliers $\lambda$ change. In those directions the base rows are equality rows like a task’s, with their Jacobian taken from $M_{\rm base}$ and, in place of a task velocity, the momentum $(M\dot q)_{\rm base}$ conjugate to the base coordinates, the total momentum of the robot. Placed at the top of the stack with the contact rows, they make Sections SEC(sec-hierarchy)–SEC(sec-power) apply to the enlarged stack, and on persistent contacts the power that the actuators inject on top of $\tau_{\rm imp}$ is $\lambda^\top\dot x+\lambda_{\rm base}^\top(M\dot q)_{\rm base}$, not $\lambda^\top\dot x$.

In both cases the sign and friction-cone conditions below bind $f_c$, not $\lambda_c$. The contact wrenches are the multipliers of the contact constraints in the plant’s own Gauss principle (of a contact that slides with friction, only $f_n$ is; see “Contact modes” below), with the applied joint torque as the impressed torque. They are constraint forces supplied by the environment, not by the actuators, yet the Gauss function EQ(gaussfn) counts them: they are part of $\tau_c=M(\ddot q-\ddot q_{\rm free})$, as the unilateral forces of EQ(unikkt) are. The centroidal momentum, finally, is part of the mechanical momentum $M\dot q$, not of the costate momentum $\pi=-\nabla_zV$ of Section SEC(sec-second-order). The two coincide (up to sign, $\pi_v=-Mv$ with $v=\dot q$ along solutions) for the infinitesimal window, where $\pi=-\nabla_zW$, when $W$ is of energy type EQ(energyW), which certifies only damping (Section SEC(sec-strict)). Over a finite window $V_\vartheta$ is in general not of energy type: in the example of Section SEC(sec-mpc-limit), $\pi_v=-\nabla_vV_\vartheta$ has the position component $-(P_\vartheta)_{12}\,q$ that gives the stiffness there.

A contact is an inequality constraint with three parts.

  • Normal complementarity. Gap $x_c\ge0$, normal force $f_n\ge0$, $f_nx_c=0$ (Signorini), imposed on a closed contact at the velocity level (Moreau) or the acceleration level (Gauss) as in Section SEC(sec-unilateral). Persistent contacts do no work; with Moreau’s impact law, impacts lose energy for restitution coefficients up to one (Moreau, 1988; Brogliato, 2016), a property of that law, not of every restitution model.
  • Friction. Coulomb’s law $\|f_t\|\le\mu_cf_n$, with tangential force $f_t$ and friction coefficient $\mu_c$, is a second-order cone, not a polyhedron; QP-based controllers replace it by an inscribed pyramid, that is, by more linear inequality rows. Sticking contacts do no work; a sliding contact with $f_t=-\mu_cf_n\,v_t/\|v_t\|$ dissipates the power $\mu_cf_n\|v_t\|\ge0$.
  • Contact modes. A mode says which contacts are closed and which of them stick or slide. In a mode without frictional sliding (every closed contact frictionless, or sticking with its force in the friction cone) the active contact constraints are ideal: their forces lie in the span of the transposed constrained rows, as in EQ(unikkt). Sections SEC(sec-hierarchy), SEC(sec-ineq-hier) and SEC(sec-power) then apply to them verbatim: EQ(forcebasis)–EQ(power), the attribution rules and the sign caveats. On a floating base the stack is the one described above (the contact rows, the base rows where they bind, and the tasks), with $f_c$ and the actuator power read as stated there. A contact that slides with friction is not ideal. For a point contact, write $J_n$ and $J_t$ for the normal and tangential rows of its Jacobian $J_c$, $v_t=J_t\dot q$ and $\hat v_t=v_t/\|v_t\|$. Only the normal acceleration $J_n\ddot q$ is constrained, while the force $(J_n-\mu_c\hat v_t^\top J_t)^\top f_n$ is not in the span of $J_n^\top$, so Gauss’s principle EQ(gauss), which would apply $J_n^\top f_n$, no longer yields the motion. The friction torque $-\mu_cJ_t^\top\hat v_t\,f_n$ can be booked as impressed instead, but it depends on the multiplier $f_n$, so the free acceleration in EQ(gauss) is no longer given. Eliminating $\ddot q$ maps the multipliers to the constrained accelerations through $JM^{-1}\tilde J^\top$, where $\tilde J$ is $J$ with each sliding row $J_n$ replaced by $J_n-\mu_c\hat v_t^\top J_t$. This matrix takes the place of the mobility $JM^{-1}J^\top$ of EQ(ldl), and it is not symmetric once another row is active. For a single sliding contact and no other rows it is the scalar $J_nM^{-1}(J_n-\mu_c\hat v_t^\top J_t)^\top$, which can be negative when $\mu_c$ is large; the contact problem then has no solution or two (Painlevé’s paradox; Brogliato, 2016). When the multipliers exist, EQ(forcebasis)–EQ(power) hold only for the ideal part $J^\top\lambda$ of the constraint torque. The friction torque, booked as impressed, is attributed to no level, and its power $-\mu_cf_n\|v_t\|$ is missing from $\lambda^\top\dot x$ in EQ(power), to which the sliding row contributes $f_nJ_n\dot q=0$. Opening a frictionless or sticking contact (by the cone, its tangential force vanishes with $f_n$) and closing a frictionless one without impact are active-set switches in the sense of Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch); across them the torque stays continuous only in the constraint view. Stick–slip transitions and the closing of a frictional contact are not. A frictional contact that closes without impact generally starts by sliding. At the onset of slip the tangential rows leave the active set with their multiplier on the boundary of the cone, not at zero. At a slip-to-stick transition the friction force in general jumps from the boundary of the cone into its interior (for a block that slides to rest on a level floor with no tangential load, from $\mu_c$ times its weight to zero), so the contact force and, in the constraint view, the torque jump without any impact.

A careful treatment of frictional sliding, impacts, friction-cone switching and tank bookkeeping across contact modes is left for later.
