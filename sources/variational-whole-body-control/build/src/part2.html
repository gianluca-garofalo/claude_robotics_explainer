SECNUM The Continuous Limit: Hamilton–Jacobi, Momentum and the Certificate

Sections SEC(sec-core) and SEC(sec-proof) worked with steps. What happens when the step itself shrinks to zero? The recursion EQ(bellman) becomes a partial differential equation, its minimization a Legendre transform, and the one-step condition EQ(V1V0) a pointwise inequality. The limit is worth taking for two reasons: it exposes the mechanics, since momentum and Hamiltonian appear without being put in, and it is where controllers that re-plan at every tick of a fast loop live.

SECNUM From steps to rates

Measure the window in time. Write $\vartheta=t_f-t$ for the time to go and $V_\vartheta(q)$ for the least action EQ(action) over paths of duration $\vartheta$ from $q$; this is EQ(VN) with the sum replaced by the integral, and $V_0$ is the boundary term of EQ(bellman). The remaining action is thus a function of two variables, the point and the time to go; when both change we write $V(q,\vartheta):=V_\vartheta(q)$. Apply the principle of optimality to a first step of length $h$, taken with a constant velocity $v$: its action is $h\,L(q,v)+O(h^2)$, the rectangle rule EQ(rect), and it arrives at $q+hv$ with $\vartheta-h$ still to go, so $$V_\vartheta(q)=\min_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big\{h\,L(q,v)+V_{\vartheta-h}(q+hv)\big\}+o(h).$$ The step changes both arguments of $V$. Along the line $t'\mapsto(q+t'v,\ \vartheta-t')$, $0\le t'\le h$, the chain rule gives the rate $\tfrac{d}{dt'}V(q+t'v,\vartheta-t')\big|_{t'=0}=\nabla V_\vartheta(q)\cdot v-\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta(q)$, so where $V$ is continuously differentiable in $(q,\vartheta)$, $$V_{\vartheta-h}(q+hv)=V_\vartheta(q)+h\,\big[\nabla V_\vartheta(q)\cdot v-\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta(q)\big]+o(h).$$ Moving to a new point changes the remaining action at the rate $\nabla V\cdot v$, and the shrinking window at the rate $-\partial_\vartheta V$. The term $V_\vartheta(q)$ cancels on both sides and $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta(q)$ does not depend on $v$; dividing by $h$ and letting $h\to0$ gives

$$ \partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta(q)=\min_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big[L(q,v)+\nabla V_\vartheta(q)\cdot v\big],\qquad V_0=W\ \text{on }\Omega . \tag{EQDEF(hjraw)} $$

This is the Hamilton–Jacobi equation (in optimal control, the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation), in time-to-go form: the rate at which the remaining action changes as the window opens. The bracket is a balance: $\nabla V\cdot v$ rewards moving downhill on what remains to be paid, while $L$ charges for moving at all. (Passing the minimizer to the limit uses remainders that are $o(h)$ uniformly over bounded sets of velocities, to which superlinearity of $L$ confines the near-minimizers, and a minimizer that exists, by superlinearity or a compact $\mathcal V(q)$, and is unique, by strict convexity of $L$ in $v$ on a convex $\mathcal V(q)$. Where $V$ is not differentiable, constraints being the usual cause, EQ(hjraw) holds in a generalized, viscosity, sense; Liberzon, 2012, §5.3.)

SECNUM Momentum and the Legendre transform

Give a name to the vector that multiplies $v$ in the bracket. The momentum field is minus the gradient of the remaining action,

$$ p(q):=-\nabla V_\vartheta(q). \tag{EQDEF(pdef)} $$

The bracket becomes $L-p\cdot v$, and its minimum over $v$ is minus a Legendre transform:

$$ \min_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big[L(q,v)-p\cdot v\big]=-H_{\mathcal V}(q,p),\qquad H_{\mathcal V}(q,p):=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big[\,p\cdot v-L(q,v)\,\big]. \tag{EQDEF(legendre)} $$

Without constraints, $\mathcal V(q)=\mathbb R^n$, we write $H$: it is the Hamiltonian of $L$. By superlinearity $p\cdot v-L(q,v)\to-\infty$ as $|v|\to\infty$, so the supremum is finite and attained, at the velocity where $\partial L/\partial v=p$. So the selected velocity is the one whose momentum $\partial L/\partial v$ equals $-\nabla V$; strict convexity of $L$ in $v$ makes $v\mapsto\partial L/\partial v$ one-to-one and superlinearity makes it onto, so this relation can be inverted for every momentum. With EQ(legendre), EQ(hjraw) reads

$$ \partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta=-H_{\mathcal V}\big(q,-\nabla V_\vartheta\big),\qquad V_0=W\ \text{on }\Omega, \tag{EQDEF(hj)} $$

or, in the absolute initial time $t=t_f-\vartheta$ with the final time $t_f$ fixed, $\partial_t\big[V_{t_f-t}(q)\big]=H_{\mathcal V}(q,-\nabla V)$. The minus sign in EQ(pdef) comes from reading the remaining action as a function of the initial point of the path still to be travelled; textbook mechanics reads the action at the final point (Section SEC(sec-characteristics) shows that the two are one rule). Everything in this section holds with or without constraints; Section SEC(sec-ineq1) collects what changes when a constraint binds, and until then we drop the subscript.

For the example EQ(example), the supremum of $pv-\tfrac12mv^2$ is attained at $v=p/m$, so $H(q,p)=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}-\tfrac12\kappa q^2$; for the quadratic Lagrangian, likewise, $H(q,p)=\tfrac12p^\top R^{-1}p-\tfrac12q^\top Qq$, attained at $v=R^{-1}p$.

The sign of the costate depends on convention. In Pontryagin’s maximum principle (normal case, cost multiplier $-1$) the costate equals the momentum $p$. In the minimum principle commonly used in MPC texts it equals $\nabla V=-p$, written $\eta$ here, and the control Hamiltonian $L+\eta\cdot v$ is minimized: $\min_v[L+\eta\cdot v]=-H(q,-\eta)$. This document fixes $p=\partial L/\partial v$ throughout.

Why $L$ cannot be dropped. Strict convexity of $L$ in $v$ is what lets the momentum determine the velocity. Set $L\equiv0$: the bracket becomes $\nabla V\cdot v$, which is linear in $v$. A Lagrangian linear in the velocities is singular: its Legendre map is not invertible, and momentum no longer determines velocity. If $\mathcal V$ is unbounded, the minimum does not exist (the speed grows without bound); if $\mathcal V$ is compact, the minimizer sits on its boundary, typically at a vertex (extreme-speed, bang-bang motion). A Lagrangian that is strictly convex and superlinear in $v$ regularizes the problem: it makes the momentum–velocity relation invertible and selects an interior velocity that balances the rate of descent against the cost of motion. For a robot the torque cost plays this role (Section SEC(sec-so-manipulator)). Both properties are needed. $L=\tfrac12\kappa q^2+\sqrt{1+v^2}-1$ is strictly convex in $v$ but grows only linearly: its momentum $\partial L/\partial v=v/\sqrt{1+v^2}$ stays in $(-1,1)$, $H(q,p)=+\infty$ for $|p|>1$, and where $|\nabla V|\ge1$ the bracket has no minimizer and the speed grows without bound, as for $L\equiv0$. In general, $H(q,\cdot)$ is finite for every momentum exactly when $L$ is superlinear in $v$.

SECNUM The receding window in continuous time

The receding-window scheme applies the first move of the window problem and re-solves. In continuous time the first move is the minimizer $v^*_\vartheta(q)$ of the bracket in EQ(hjraw), and the closed loop is $\dot q=v^*_\vartheta(q)$ with the window length $\vartheta$ held fixed. Along it, by EQ(hjraw) evaluated at its own minimizer,

$$ \frac{d}{dt}V_\vartheta\big(q(t)\big)=\nabla V_\vartheta\cdot v^*_\vartheta=-L\big(q,v^*_\vartheta\big)+\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta(q). \tag{EQDEF(rhcrate)} $$

So the continuous-time descent condition $\dot V_\vartheta\le-L$ holds exactly where $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\le0$, that is, where a longer window would not raise the remaining action. This is EQ(monotone) and EQ(descent) in continuous time. Descent at the full rate $L$ is more decrease than a Lyapunov function needs: by EQ(rhcrate), $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\le(1-\beta)\,L(q,v^*_\vartheta)$ for all $q$, with a constant $0<\beta\le1$, gives $\dot V_\vartheta\le-\beta L\le-\beta\,\alpha_L(|q|)$, the continuous counterpart of EQ(strictdescent) with $\beta\,\alpha_L$ in place of $\alpha_L$. The remaining action can therefore decrease along the receding window although it increases with the window length; the scaled candidates $W=cV_\infty$ with $0<c<1$ do so at $\vartheta=0$ (Section SEC(sec-stationary)) and, on the hilltop, for every window (Section SEC(sec-scalar)). In steps the bookkeeping is the same: by EQ(bellman) at its minimizer, $V_N(q^+)-V_N(q)=-L_d(q,q^+)+\big[V_N(q^+)-V_{N-1}(q^+)\big]$, so EQ(descent) holds exactly when $V_N(q^+)\le V_{N-1}(q^+)$, while decrease needs only a bracket below $L_d(q,q^+)$ (Section SEC(sec-numeric)). The full rate is the one pursued here because it can be checked on the pair $(L,W)$ before any window is solved (Sections SEC(sec-infinitesimal) and SEC(sec-allwindows)), whereas the weaker condition involves $V_\vartheta$ itself. What remains is to find boundary terms for which $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\le0$, and the place to look is the shortest window.

SECNUM The infinitesimal window: a rule and a certificate

As $\vartheta\to0$, $V_\vartheta\to W$ on $\Omega$ (by superlinearity, a path that covers a fixed distance in a vanishing time has unbounded action, and $W$ is continuous): the boundary term itself plays the part of the remaining action, and the receding window applies the minimizer of the bracket with $V=W$,

$$ v^*(q)=\arg\min_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big\{L(q,v)+\nabla W(q)\cdot v\big\}. \tag{EQDEF(rule)} $$

The window problem has become a pointwise feedback law: one minimization at the current state, with no trajectory to optimize. A controller that re-plans at every tick of a fast loop, with a window as short as one tick, lives in this limit, and it is the form whole-body controllers take; for the arm of Section SEC(sec-robot) it will be PD control with gravity compensation. By EQ(legendre) with $p=-\nabla W$, or by EQ(rhcrate) at $\vartheta=0$, the rate of change of $W$ along the rule is

$$ \dot W=\nabla W\cdot v^*=-L(q,v^*)-H\big(q,-\nabla W(q)\big). \tag{EQDEF(wdot)} $$

Hence the continuous-time descent condition $\dot W\le -L$ holds if and only if

$$ \min_{v}\big[L(q,v)+\dot W(q,v)\big]\le0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H\big(q,-\nabla W(q)\big)\ \ge\ 0 , \tag{EQDEF(cert)} $$

with $\dot W(q,v)=\nabla W(q)\cdot v$. This is the continuous form of EQ(terminal): the rule EQ(rule) plays the part of the reference flow, and EQ(cert) says that along it $W$ decreases at least at the rate at which action is spent. By EQ(hj) it is also the continuous form of EQ(V1V0): $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\big|_{\vartheta=0}=-H(q,-\nabla W)\le0$, so a short window never needs more than $W$. Here $W$ is a candidate, and EQ(cert) is its certificate.

The same conclusions follow from the action of a short window directly. Over a window of length $\vartheta$ with constant velocity $v$, both terms of the action expand to first order in $\vartheta$:

$$ \begin{gathered} S_\vartheta(q,v)=\int_t^{t+\vartheta}L\,dt'+W(q+\vartheta v)\\ =W(q)+\vartheta\,\big[\,L(q,v)+\nabla W(q)\cdot v\,\big]+o(\vartheta). \end{gathered} \tag{EQDEF(expansion)} $$

$W(q)$ does not depend on $v$. Subtracting it and dividing by $\vartheta>0$ do not change which $v$ minimizes, and both steps are needed: as $\vartheta\to0$, $S_\vartheta\to W(q)$ for every $v$, so the limit of $S_\vartheta$ itself cannot rank velocities, whose costs differ only by $O(\vartheta)$. The comparison of costs $V_1\le W$ of Section SEC(sec-proof) degenerates to $W\le W$; what survives is the comparison of rates, the bracket, which is what a Lyapunov argument checks. With $v=v^*$, EQ(expansion) bounds the remaining action of the infinitesimal window from above by $W-\vartheta\,H(q,-\nabla W)+o(\vartheta)$. Conversely, along any admissible path $W\big(q(\vartheta)\big)=W(q)+\int_0^\vartheta\nabla W\cdot\dot q\,dt$, so its action is $W(q)+\int_0^\vartheta\big[L+\nabla W\cdot\dot q\big]\,dt\ge W(q)-\int_0^\vartheta H\big(q(t),-\nabla W(q(t))\big)\,dt$, and superlinearity keeps the paths of bounded action within $o(1)$ of $q$. Hence, at interior points of $\Omega$, $V_\vartheta=W-\vartheta\,H(q,-\nabla W)+o(\vartheta)$, the first-order solution of EQ(hj).

Two outputs of one minimization. The bracket $L+\dot W$ is minimized once and yields two different objects: its argmin EQ(rule), the controller the rule selects, and its minimum value $-H(q,-\nabla W)$, whose sign is the certificate EQ(cert). They answer different questions. A convergent controller whose certificate fails is possible and common; the first examples follow in Sections SEC(sec-stationary) and SEC(sec-numeric). The certificate is a property of the pair $(L,W)$, not of $W$ alone and not of the closed loop.

SECNUM One certificate for every window length

Section SEC(sec-rhc) reduced descent of the receding window to $\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\le0$, and Section SEC(sec-infinitesimal) gave that inequality at $\vartheta=0$. It propagates to every $\vartheta$ by the argument of Section SEC(sec-monotone):

  1. A short window never needs more than $W$. Suppose EQ(cert) holds on $\Omega$ and the rule EQ(rule) keeps $\Omega$ invariant (for instance $\Omega$ a sublevel set of $W$, which $\dot W\le-L\le0$ keeps invariant). Integrating $L+\dot W=-H(q,-\nabla W)\le0$ along the rule over a time $\vartheta'$ gives $\int_0^{\vartheta'} L\,dt+W\big(q(\vartheta')\big)\le W(q)$: a feasible path for the window $\vartheta'$ that costs no more than $W$. Hence $V_{\vartheta'}\le V_0$.
  2. Monotonicity in the boundary term. The principle of optimality, splitting the window $\vartheta+\vartheta'$ after its first $\vartheta$, writes $V_{\vartheta+\vartheta'}$ as the window-$\vartheta$ problem with boundary term $V_{\vartheta'}$. That problem is monotone in its boundary term, exactly as $\mathcal B$ is, so $V_{\vartheta'}\le V_0$ gives
$$ \begin{gathered} V_{\vartheta+\vartheta'}\le V_\vartheta\quad\text{for all }\vartheta,\vartheta'\ge0,\\ \text{hence}\quad\partial_\vartheta V_\vartheta\le0 . \end{gathered} \tag{EQDEF(ctsmonotone)} $$

By EQ(rhcrate), the receding window then satisfies $\dot V_\vartheta\le-L$ for every window length: one condition on the pair $(L,W)$ certifies all of them, as EQ(terminal) did in discrete time. In receding-horizon control this is the standard continuous-time requirement on a terminal cost that is a control Lyapunov function (Chen & Allgöwer, 1998; Jadbabaie et al., 2001), and the rule EQ(rule) is a pointwise controller built on that function, the kind of law that Primbs et al. (2000) generalize to receding horizons.

SECNUM The exact boundary term: the stationary Hamilton–Jacobi equation

Equality in EQ(cert) for every $q$ makes the right-hand side of EQ(hj) vanish at $\vartheta=0$, and $W$ is a stationary solution of EQ(hj); whether it is the remaining action depends on the terminal region. By EQ(legendre), along any admissible path $L+\dot W\ge\min_{v\in\mathcal V(q)}\big[L+\nabla W\cdot v\big]=-H(q,-\nabla W)=0$, with equality only at the minimizer EQ(rule). Integrated over the window, this says that every path from $q$ that ends in $\Omega$ costs at least $W(q)$, and that the path of the rule EQ(rule) attains $W(q)$ whenever it ends in $\Omega$. Without a terminal constraint ($\Omega=\mathbb R^n$, so $V_0=W$) this gives $V_\vartheta=W$ for all $\vartheta$, and the window length is irrelevant. With a terminal region, $V_\vartheta=W$ exactly where the path of the rule is in $\Omega$ at time $\vartheta$: if the rule keeps $\Omega$ invariant, on $\Omega$ and at every point that the rule brings into $\Omega$ within time $\vartheta$, a set that grows with $\vartheta$. Elsewhere the terminal constraint binds and $V_\vartheta>W$, and the window length still sets the value, the first move and, with bounded velocities, the solvable set (numbers in Section SEC(sec-scalar)). The condition, equality in EQ(cert) for every $q$, is the stationary Hamilton–Jacobi equation,

$$ H\big(q,-\nabla V_\infty(q)\big)=0 . \tag{EQDEF(stationary)} $$

Among continuously differentiable $W\ge0$ with $W(0)=0$ (and assuming every finite-action path converges to the rest point), its solution is exactly the infinite-time remaining action, $W=V_\infty$; this is the verification theorem of optimal control (Liberzon, 2012, Sec. 5.1). The boundary term is then exact. Without these qualifications the equation has other solutions (for the quadratic case below, $-V_\infty$).

Quadratic Lagrangians. For $L=\tfrac12q^\top Qq+\tfrac12v^\top Rv$, try $W=\tfrac12q^\top Xq$ with $X\succ0$. Then $p=-\nabla W=-Xq$, and the Hamiltonian of Section SEC(sec-momentum) gives

$$ H\big(q,-Xq\big)=\tfrac12\,q^\top\big(XR^{-1}X-Q\big)\,q,\qquad v^*=R^{-1}p=-R^{-1}Xq . \tag{EQDEF(quadH)} $$

The stationary equation EQ(stationary) holds if and only if $XR^{-1}X=Q$. Its positive-definite solution $P$, $PR^{-1}P=Q$, is the continuous-time Riccati solution for $\dot q=v$; with $W=\tfrac12q^\top Pq$ the rule EQ(rule) is the linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) $v=-R^{-1}Pq$. For the example EQ(example), $P=\sqrt{m\kappa}$.

Example: scaled candidates. Take $W=c\,V_\infty$, i.e. $X=cP$ with $c>0$. By EQ(quadH) and $PR^{-1}P=Q$, $H(q,-\nabla W)=\tfrac{c^2-1}{2}\,q^\top Qq$:
  • $c=1$: $H=0$, and $\dot W=-L$ exactly;
  • $c>1$: $H>0$, so $\dot W<-L$: an over-estimate of the tail certifies descent;
  • $c<1$: $H<0$, so $\dot W>-L$. $W$ still decreases ($\dot W=-c^2q^\top Qq$), but EQ(cert) fails: the condition is sufficient for decrease, not necessary.
Under-estimating the tail breaks the certificate, not necessarily the convergence: the first instance of a good argmin with a failed certificate. Section SEC(sec-scalar) follows the three cases to finite windows, and Section SEC(sec-numeric) finds the discrete mirror image.
