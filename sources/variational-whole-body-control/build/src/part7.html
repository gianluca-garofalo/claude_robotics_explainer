SECNUM Two Lagrangians and the Stability of Equilibria

It is tempting to ask: if a positive-definite Lagrangian yields stability in Part I, what does the physical Lagrangian $\mathcal T-U$ say about the stability of the plant? The two questions look as if they should be linked, and the signs seem to clash: the hilltop of Section SEC(sec-scalar) is a positive-definite variational Lagrangian whose mechanical reading is an unstable equilibrium. There is no clash: it is a sign difference between two unrelated Lagrangians (the table of Section SEC(sec-so-augmented)). The section ends by placing the impressed and constraint forces of Section SEC(sec-impressed) on the two sides of this divide.

SECNUM The variational Lagrangian

A positive-definite $L$ is a necessary ingredient of the stability argument, not the argument itself. Stability follows because the remaining action $V$ is a Lyapunov function: $V\ge\alpha_1(|q|)$ and $V^+-V\le-L_d$ (Section SEC(sec-proof)), or $\dot V\le-L$ in continuous time (Section SEC(sec-hj); for the infinitesimal window, $V=W$).

SECNUM The physical Lagrangian

For a natural system, $L_{\text{phys}}=\mathcal T-U$ is positive definite only if $U$ is negative definite, which puts the origin at a potential maximum. What is known about equilibria of natural systems, with Lagrangian $\mathcal T-U$ and positive-definite kinetic energy $\mathcal T=\tfrac12\dot q^\top M(q)\dot q$, is summarized below.

StatementStatus
Strict local minimum of $U$ ⇒ Lyapunov stable (Lagrange–Dirichlet)Theorem. $E=\mathcal T+U$ is a Lyapunov function.
A negative eigenvalue of $\nabla^2U(0)$ ⇒ unstableTheorem. The linearization has a positive real eigenvalue (Lyapunov’s first method).
Local maximum of a smooth potential ⇒ Lyapunov unstableTheorem. Strict maximum: Hagedorn (1971); any maximum of a $C^2$ potential, strict or not: Taliaferro (1980a). Stronger instability notions can fail even at an isolated maximum (Ureña, 2020).
“Not a minimum ⇒ unstable” for smooth ($C^\infty$) potentialsFalse. Painlevé (1904) and Wintner gave one-degree-of-freedom counterexamples. Painlevé–Wintner-type potentials oscillate infinitely often near the origin, e.g. $U(q)=e^{-1/q^2}\cos(1/q)$, $U(0)=0$: the origin is stable but not a minimum. Laloy (1976) gave a two-degree-of-freedom example.
Same statement for real-analytic potentialsIn two degrees of freedom a non-minimum critical point is unstable: Palamodov (1977) and Taliaferro (1980b) proved it for the Newtonian kinetic energy ($M=I$, $\ddot q=-\nabla U$), and Burgos & Paternain (2022, preprint, §1) report its extension to an arbitrary kinetic energy by Kozlov (1981). Laloy & Peiffer (1982) prove that a non-strict minimum is unstable as well; as Burgos & Paternain (2022, §1) note, this completes the analytic converse of Lagrange–Dirichlet (stable ⇒ strict minimum) in two degrees of freedom. In higher dimension even Arnold’s problem 1971-4, instability at an isolated critical point that is not a minimum (Arnold, 2004), is open in general. In dimension three, Burgos & Paternain (2022, preprint) prove instability on an open dense subset of non-strict minima. From three degrees of freedom on, the analytic converse of Lagrange–Dirichlet is open (see also Burgos et al., 2021).
“Stable if and only if $U$ has a strict local minimum” as a general theoremDoes not exist in this generality. The “if” half is Lagrange–Dirichlet (first row); the “only if” half is its converse. The converse is false for smooth potentials (Painlevé–Wintner, above). For real-analytic potentials it is proved in one degree of freedom and in two (previous row), and open from three on. One degree of freedom is elementary: if $0$ is not a strict minimum, either $U$ is constant near $0$, and then every motion with $\dot q\neq0$ is monotone and leaves a fixed neighbourhood, or the lowest-order term $aq^k$ of $U-U(0)$ has $k$ odd or $a<0$, so $U$ decreases strictly away from $0$ on one side, and a motion released at rest on that side, however close to $0$, runs downhill out of a fixed neighbourhood. Without “strict” even the “if” half fails for analytic $U$: for $U\equiv0$ and $M=I$, $q(t)=q_0+\dot q_0t$ with $\dot q_0\neq0$ leaves every neighbourhood of $0$.
A potential maximum is always unstableFalse once the system is not purely natural (next two rows).
Gyroscopic forces can stabilize a maximumA linear (spectral) statement. For the linearization $M\ddot q+G\dot q+\nabla^2U(0)\,q=0$, with gyroscopic matrix $G=-G^\top$: an odd number of negative eigenvalues of the stiffness $\nabla^2U(0)$ (degree of instability) makes gyroscopic stabilization impossible (Thomson–Tait); an even degree is necessary, and then stabilization is possible for suitable $G$ (sufficient conditions for even dimension and negative-definite stiffness: Seyranian et al., 1995). Adding complete (positive-definite) damping destroys it (Kelvin–Tait–Chetaev). All of this is spectral: nonlinear Lyapunov stability needs further arguments. An application is the gyroscopic stabilization of orbits (Mohandas et al., 2019).
Vibrational averaging can stabilize a maximum (Kapitza pendulum)The system is driven (explicitly time-dependent), so not an autonomous natural system. For pivot amplitude $a\ll l$ and frequency $\nu\gg\omega_0=\sqrt{g_0/l}$, averaging gives an effective potential with a minimum at the upright position when $a^2\nu^2>2g_0l$, i.e. $(a/l)(\nu/\omega_0)>\sqrt2$ (Landau & Lifshitz, 1976, §30, Problem 1).

SECNUM Resolution

In Section SEC(sec-scalar) the variational problem is an unstable hilltop, and that is exactly why the optimal path is the stable manifold of a saddle: the extremal rides the separatrix. The physical stability of the plant is a separate matter, governed by the plant’s actual energy $E=\mathcal T+U$, not by the variational Lagrangian.

SECNUM Where the two meet: energy shaping

The method of controlled Lagrangians modifies the plant’s Lagrangian so that the closed loop is Lagrangian by construction, which lets energy methods select stabilizing gains.

  • Part I (Bloch et al., 2000) uses kinetic shaping and stabilizes the system modulo a symmetry group.
  • Part II (Bloch et al., 2001) adds potential shaping and feedback dissipation, giving asymptotic stabilization of the full state.

The shaped energy has a minimum at the target. Lagrange–Dirichlet then gives Lyapunov stability, and injected dissipation (with LaSalle’s invariance principle) gives asymptotic stability. This is the physical analogue of Section SEC(sec-proof), with the shaped energy playing the role of $V$.

Damping cuts both ways. Here damping helps, because the shaped energy has a minimum. In the gyroscopic row of Section SEC(sec-phys-lag) it destabilizes, because there the equilibrium is a maximum held only by gyroscopic coupling.

SECNUM Impressed and constraint forces on the two sides

The two ways of acting on a task in Section SEC(sec-two-ways) fall on opposite sides of the divide between the two Lagrangians.

  • Impressed forces shape the physical Lagrangian. A natural-inertia controller (one task, or several tasks without null-space projections) leaves the kinetic energy alone and reshapes only the potential, $\mathcal T-U\mapsto\mathcal T-U_s$ (gravity compensated, task springs added), then injects damping. Its closed loop is a mechanical system again, and its stability argument is the physical one of Section SEC(sec-shaping): Lagrange–Dirichlet for the shaped energy and LaSalle for the damping. In the language of Part I this energy is a weak boundary term (Section SEC(sec-strict)). Under strict priority the projected task forces are no longer the gradient of one potential, and the argument has to be made level by level (Section SEC(sec-leak)).
  • Constraint forces come from a variational problem. An operational-space controller does not modify the physical Lagrangian; it solves Gauss’s problem with the task as a constraint at every instant and applies the multiplier. The task then behaves as a unit mass. In a Lagrangian picture that would be a reshaping of the kinetic energy (compare the kinetic shaping of Bloch et al., 2000), but here it is produced by a constraint force computed from the model, not by a new closed-loop Lagrangian, and unmeasured external forces still meet the natural inertia (Section SEC(sec-two-ways)).
  • The receding window sits on the variational side, with a positive-definite Lagrangian and a certificate of Hamilton–Jacobi type. Section SEC(sec-robot) shows that its infinitesimal limit can land on a physical-side controller (PD with gravity compensation, the classical potential-shaping law) while the certificate stays variational.
Part III · Hierarchies, power and windows

SECNUM Hierarchy as Gram–Schmidt in the $M^{-1}$ Metric

With several tasks the question “what is the force on task $i$?” acquires a second layer: the same torque can be split among tasks in many ways. Strict priorities pick one split. This section shows that the split is a change of basis, and that one factorization produces all the objects of hierarchical, inertially decoupled control. It starts from the reason a change of basis matters at all.

SECNUM Forces are covectors

A multiplier pairs with a velocity to give a power, and only that pairing is independent of the coordinates. Change the coordinates of the velocity, and the multiplier changes by the transposed map. The arm of Section SEC(sec-robot) gives a small example: replace the velocity $v$ by the composite velocity $s=v+\Gamma e$ of EQ(armW), with $e=q-q_d$ the position error from the constant target (so ${\dot e=v}$). Writing $W(e,v)=\tilde W(e,s)$ with $v=s-\Gamma e$, the chain rule gives $\partial\tilde W/\partial s=\partial W/\partial v$ and $\partial\tilde W/\partial e=\partial W/\partial e-\Gamma^\top\partial W/\partial v$, so in the coordinates $(e,s)$ the momenta are $$\tilde\pi_s=\pi_v,\qquad\tilde\pi_e=\pi_q-\Gamma^\top\pi_v .$$ The velocity momentum, which sets the torque, is unchanged; the position momentum is not, because $\partial W/\partial e$ at fixed $v$ is not $\partial W/\partial e$ at fixed $s$. The pairing $\tilde\pi_e\cdot\dot e+\tilde\pi_s\cdot\dot s=\pi_q\cdot\dot e+\pi_v\cdot\dot v=-\dot W$ is the same in both (checked numerically for the arm, Section SEC(sec-verification)). The same mechanism appears in task space: the “force on a task” depends on which other task coordinates are held fixed, and a priority order is that choice.

SECNUM One factorization

Let task $i$ have coordinates $x_i(q)$ of dimension $n_i$ and Jacobian $J_i=\partial x_i/\partial q$, with $r$ priority levels, $i=1$ the highest. Stack the task Jacobians in priority order, $J=[J_1;\dots;J_r]$, so that the task inertia of Section SEC(sec-gauss-task) is $\Lambda=(JM^{-1}J^\top)^{-1}$, and that of task $i$ alone $\Lambda_i=(J_iM^{-1}J_i^\top)^{-1}$. Throughout, the stack $J$ has full row rank (no algorithmic singularities; every set of task accelerations is then compatible), except where rank deficiency is discussed explicitly, and the terms $\dot J_i\dot q$ are absorbed in the task accelerations. Factor the stacked mobility (the inverse of the stacked task inertia) as

$$ JM^{-1}J^\top=\Theta\,\bar\Lambda^{-1}\,\Theta^\top,\qquad \Theta=\begin{pmatrix}I&&\\ \Theta_{21}&I&\\ \vdots&&\ddots\end{pmatrix},\qquad \bar\Lambda=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\bar\Lambda_1,\dots,\bar\Lambda_r). \tag{EQDEF(ldl)} $$

This is a block $LDL^\top$ factorization, with $\Theta$ in the role of $L$ (the letter $L$ stays reserved for Lagrangians) and the pivots written as inverse inertias. It is also the block form of Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization of the rows of $J$ in the inner product $\langle a,b\rangle=aM^{-1}b^\top$: each block row is made $M^{-1}$-orthogonal to all rows above it. Define

$$ \bar J=\Theta^{-1}J,\qquad \zeta=\bar J\dot q,\qquad \dot x=J\dot q=\Theta\,\zeta . \tag{EQDEF(Jbar)} $$

SECNUM What it reproduces

  • Dynamically consistent extended Jacobians. Since $\Theta$ is unit block lower triangular, block row $i$ of $\bar J$ is $J_i$ minus its $M^{-1}$-orthogonal projection on the rows above it, $$\bar J_i=J_i\big(I-J_{<i}^{M+}J_{<i}\big),\qquad J_{<i}^{M+}=M^{-1}J_{<i}^\top\big(J_{<i}M^{-1}J_{<i}^\top\big)^{-1},\tag{EQDEF(extJ)}$$ with $J_{<i}$ the stack of higher-priority tasks. That is $J_i$ times the dynamically consistent null-space projector of the higher tasks: the projected (null-space-restricted) task Jacobian of hierarchical control (Dietrich et al., 2015; Ott et al., 2015). We call the stack $\bar J$ the extended Jacobian, following that literature; it is unrelated to Baillieul’s extended-Jacobian method. Checked numerically to $2.1\times10^{-15}$.
  • Dynamic consistency. By construction $J_{<i}M^{-1}\bar J_i^\top=0$: a torque $\bar J_i^\top F_i$ produces no acceleration of any higher-priority task.
  • Decoupled inertias. $\bar JM^{-1}\bar J^\top=\Theta^{-1}JM^{-1}J^\top\Theta^{-\top}=\bar\Lambda^{-1}$ is block diagonal, so $\bar\Lambda_i$ is the inertia seen by level $i$. If the stack is square ($\sum_i n_i=n$, completing it with a null-space task if needed), the kinetic energy splits: $$\tfrac12\,\dot q^\top M\dot q=\sum_i\tfrac12\,\zeta_i^\top\bar\Lambda_i\,\zeta_i .\tag{EQDEF(KEsplit)}$$ This is the inertially decoupled structure exploited by hierarchical passivity-based controllers (Ott et al., 2015; Dietrich & Ott, 2020; Garofalo & Ott, 2020; Garofalo, 2024).
  • Conflicts and singularities. If a level is (partly) in conflict with higher levels, its pivot $\bar J_iM^{-1}\bar J_i^\top$ is singular. Using pseudo-inverse pivots in EQ(ldl) still reproduces EQ(extJ) with pseudo-inverses, and $\bar J$ stays unique; $\Theta$ is then not unique, neither are $\lambda$ and $F=\Theta^\top\lambda$ (Section SEC(sec-forces)), and $\bar\Lambda_i$ exists only as a pseudo-inverse. Rank deficiency of the stack is a property of the Jacobians; whether the task targets are then compatible is a separate question, which decides whether the lower task is met or only satisfied in a least-squares sense.
Inertial decoupling is not dynamic decoupling. For a square stack and $\tau=g+\bar J^\top F$, the dynamics in the decoupled velocities read $\bar\Lambda\dot\zeta+\bar C\,\zeta=F$, with $\bar C$ the Coriolis matrix in these coordinates and $\dot{\bar\Lambda}-2\bar C$ skew-symmetric. Since $\bar\Lambda$ is block diagonal, the off-diagonal blocks satisfy $\bar C_{ij}=-\bar C_{ji}^\top$, and $$\frac{d}{dt}\,\tfrac12\,\zeta_i^\top\bar\Lambda_i\zeta_i=\zeta_i^\top F_i-\sum_{j\ne i}\zeta_i^\top\bar C_{ij}\zeta_j .$$ The Coriolis couplings exchange kinetic energy between levels without creating any (the coupling terms cancel in the sum over $i$). Hierarchical controllers compensate them or carry them through the proof (Ott et al., 2015; Dietrich & Ott, 2020). For Section SEC(sec-power) this means that $F_i^\top\zeta_i$ is the power injected into level $i$, not the rate of change of its kinetic energy.
The factorization is the computational content; the result is not new. Successive dynamically consistent projection is the standard construction, and sparse factorizations of the joint-space inertia matrix make the operational-space inertia and the dynamically consistent projectors cheap to compute (Featherstone, 2010); in prioritized whole-body control the decoupled inertias $\bar\Lambda_i$ are the prioritized task inertias (Sentis & Khatib, 2005). The dual statement of the next subsection, the change of basis $F=\Theta^\top\lambda$ between decoupled forces and stacked multipliers, follows directly from the velocity relation EQ(Jbar); we make it explicit because it organizes the rest of Part III.

SECNUM Forces on a task depend on the basis

Write $\tau_x$ for the torque produced through the task channel. In the constraint view it is the constraint torque $\tau_c$ of Section SEC(sec-gauss-task), with the stacked multipliers $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)$ of the stacked constraints; in the impressed view it is the task part of $\tau_{\rm imp}$ ($J^\top F$ of Section SEC(sec-two-ways), and $\bar J^\top F$ level by level, with decoupled task forces $F=(F_1,\dots,F_r)$; a single task is the case $r=1$, $\bar J=J$). In both views it can be written in two ways: through the stacked Jacobian with stacked multipliers, or through the extended Jacobians with decoupled forces. Since $J=\Theta\bar J$, the transpose of the velocity relation EQ(Jbar) gives

$$ \tau_x=J^\top\lambda=\bar J^\top F \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad F=\Theta^\top\lambda,\qquad F_i=\lambda_i+\sum_{j>i}\Theta_{ji}^\top\lambda_j . \tag{EQDEF(forcebasis)} $$

In the constraint view, $\lambda$ are the multipliers of the stacked Gauss problem EQ(osc); in the impressed view, $F$ are the forces the controller designs per level. Equation EQ(forcebasis) is the dictionary between them. It is Newton’s law written in two coordinate systems: multiplying $\tau_x=J^\top\lambda$ and $\tau_x=\bar J^\top F$ by $JM^{-1}$ and $\bar JM^{-1}$ and using EQ(ldl),

$$ \lambda=\Lambda\,\big(JM^{-1}\tau_x\big),\qquad F_i=\bar\Lambda_i\,\big(\bar J_iM^{-1}\tau_x\big): \tag{EQDEF(forcesacc)} $$

each is an inertia times the acceleration that $\tau_x$ produces, $\lambda$ in the stacked task coordinates with their coupled inertia $\Lambda$, and $F_i$ in the decoupled coordinate of level $i$ with its decoupled inertia $\bar\Lambda_i$. This is the covector mechanism of Section SEC(sec-covectors): a component of a force depends on which other coordinates are held fixed. Three consequences:

  • The effort separates by level (in the constraint view, the Gauss function): $\tfrac12\tau_x^\top M^{-1}\tau_x=\tfrac12F^\top\bar JM^{-1}\bar J^\top F=\sum_i\tfrac12F_i^\top\bar\Lambda_i^{-1}F_i$.
  • Only the last level agrees: $F_r=\lambda_r$. Every higher level carries, in addition, the multipliers of the lower levels mapped through $\Theta$.
  • The priority order is a choice of basis. With the same torque and the order reversed, the force attributed to a task in general changes; it cannot for a task with $J_iM^{-1}J_k^\top=0$ for all $k\neq i$ (see the end of this subsection). In a fixed-seed example (random symmetric positive-definite $M$, Jacobians and stacked multipliers $\lambda$, $n=7$, task dimensions 2, 3, 2, and a random joint velocity $\dot q$ for the powers of Section SEC(sec-invariance)), task 1 carries $F_1=(-0.079,\,-1.197)$ at first priority and $(0.260,\,-0.858)$ at last priority, which equals its stacked multiplier.
What “the force acting on a task” means. There is no basis-free answer. The multiplier $\lambda_i$ is the force on task $i$ as seen in the stacked task coordinates; $F_i$ is the force on task $i$ in the decoupled coordinates of the hierarchy. They coincide, in general, only for the lowest-priority task. In the constraint view with a feasible stack, all orders produce the same torque and the same acceleration; the order then matters for the motion only under conflict or singularity (including conflicts that active inequalities create, Section SEC(sec-ineq-hier)), but it matters, in general, for the attribution of forces. By EQ(forcesacc), task $i$ carries $F_i=\Lambda_iJ_iM^{-1}\tau_x=\lambda_i+\Lambda_i\sum_{k\neq i}J_iM^{-1}J_k^\top\lambda_k$ at first priority (where $\bar J_i=J_i$ and $\bar\Lambda_i=\Lambda_i$) and $F_i=\lambda_i$ at last priority. For generic multipliers the two differ unless $J_iM^{-1}J_k^\top=0$ for all $k\neq i$; in that case EQ(extJ) gives $\bar J_i=J_i$, hence $F_i=\lambda_i$, in every order. In the impressed view, where the designer prescribes each $F_i$, the order in general changes the torque itself, because $\bar J$ changes with it (checked on a feasible stack, Section SEC(sec-verification)). Neither effect occurs if the stacked task inertia $\Lambda$ is already block diagonal ($J_iM^{-1}J_k^\top=0$ for all $i\neq k$; for example, hand tasks on two arms mounted on a fixed base with no joint in common): then $\bar J=J$, $\Theta=I$ and $F=\lambda$ in every order.

SECNUM Strict priority and the leak

Suppose a lower-priority task $i$ wants to apply a force $f_i$, for example $f_i=-\nabla U_i(x_i)$ from its potential $U_i(x_i)$. Written with its own Jacobian, the torque spreads over all levels at and above $i$: by EQ(Jbar), $J_i=\sum_{j\le i}\Theta_{ij}\bar J_j$ (with $\Theta_{ii}=I$), so

$$ J_i^\top f_i=\sum_{j\le i}\bar J_j^\top \Theta_{ij}^\top f_i . \tag{EQDEF(leak)} $$

The terms $j<i$ disturb higher-priority tasks. Strict priority keeps only $j=i$, i.e. applies $\bar J_i^\top f_i$. This has a consequence for the variational picture: the resulting torque is in general not the gradient of a potential on $q$, because the projected Jacobians are not integrable. There is then no single potential, and hence no single $W$ of the form EQ(energyW), whose descent certifies the whole hierarchy. The stability analyses of hierarchical passivity-based controllers accordingly work level by level and handle the remaining couplings explicitly (Ott et al., 2015; Dietrich & Ott, 2020; Garofalo & Ott, 2020). In the language of Part I, strict priority is lexicographic (as in hierarchical quadratic programming; Escande et al., 2014), not the argmin of one remaining action.

SECNUM Inequality Tasks in a Hierarchy

Joint limits, obstacle distances and contact conditions enter whole-body control as inequality tasks, at any priority level. This section extends Section SEC(sec-hierarchy) to them, with the unilateral forces of Section SEC(sec-unilateral) as the building block.

SECNUM The lexicographic problem and its active set

A strict hierarchy with inequalities is a lexicographic optimization: level by level, minimize the violation of that level’s equalities and inequalities while keeping the optimal violations of all levels above, with Gauss’s function closing the hierarchy as the last level (Kanoun et al., 2011; Escande et al., 2014). Two facts organize the rest of this section.

  • For a fixed active set it is an equality hierarchy. Active inequality rows (the set $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal I$ held at their bounds) are kept, inactive ones are dropped, and Section SEC(sec-hierarchy) applies to the active stack $J_{\mathcal A}$ (the active rows inserted at their levels): one factorization $J_{\mathcal A}M^{-1}J_{\mathcal A}^\top=\Theta_{\mathcal A}\bar\Lambda_{\mathcal A}^{-1}\Theta_{\mathcal A}^\top$, extended Jacobians $\bar J_{\mathcal A}=\Theta_{\mathcal A}^{-1}J_{\mathcal A}$ and decoupled forces $F=\Theta_{\mathcal A}^\top\lambda_{\mathcal A}$.
  • A feasible stack is a single QP. If all tasks can be met at once, the lexicographic solution is the Gauss problem with every row as a constraint, EQ(unikkt); the order then decides only the attribution, as in Section SEC(sec-forces). The multipliers carry the signs of EQ(unikkt), and the solution of this strictly convex QP depends continuously on its data as long as the active rows stay linearly independent.

Garofalo (2024) uses this structure in the passivity-based setting: a weighted hierarchical QP extracts the set of active tasks, and the stack of active tasks defines the coordinate transformation that inertially decouples them, with the natural inertia preserved.

SECNUM Activation and deactivation at any level

An inequality row switches in two ways, both read off its complementary pair in EQ(unikkt), its multiplier and its margin (its entry of $J_{\mathcal I}\ddot q-a_{\mathcal I}$): it activates when its margin reaches zero while its multiplier is still zero, and it deactivates when its multiplier returns to zero while its margin is still zero. These are events at the acceleration level, not at the position level: with an acceleration bound that is a feedback law (Section SEC(sec-unilateral)) a row can become active before its coordinate reaches its position bound, and it can be active while its coordinate recedes, as in the example of Section SEC(sec-unilateral). At the switching instant both complementary quantities vanish, so the solution with the row dropped, given a zero multiplier for the row, also satisfies the KKT conditions with the row held at its bound; by strict convexity the two solutions coincide there. Hence in the constraint view, for a feasible stack whose data $M$, $\ddot q_{\rm free}$, $J_{\mathcal E}$, $J_{\mathcal I}$, $a_{\mathcal E}$, $a_{\mathcal I}$ vary continuously in time and whose active rows stay linearly independent (Section SEC(sec-lexi)), the torque is continuous across the switch. This excludes impacts. A row imposed only on the boundary, as for a contact, enters the problem without a jump only if its coordinate arrives there with zero velocity: a contact that closes with nonzero approach velocity cannot be kept from penetrating by any finite force, and the impulse it needs makes the velocity, and in general the data of EQ(unikkt) and the torque, jump. Impacts are not treated here (Sections SEC(sec-unilateral) and SEC(sec-outlook)). The active stack, and with it the basis of Section SEC(sec-hierarchy), changes discontinuously. Since Gram–Schmidt orthogonalizes each level only against the levels above it, a switch at level $i$ leaves $\bar J_j$, $\zeta_j$ and $\Theta_{kj}$ for $j<i$, and hence by EQ(forcesacc) the decoupled forces and powers of every level above $i$, untouched; it re-orthogonalizes level $i$ and everything below.

In a fixed-seed example ($n=7$; random inertia, Jacobians and free acceleration, and a random joint velocity $\dot q$ for the powers; a two-row equality task at level 1; at level 2 a one-row inequality $J_2\ddot q\ge a_2(t)$, an upper bound on a task coordinate $x_2$ imposed at the acceleration level, $J_2=-\partial x_2/\partial q$; a three-row equality task at level 3; feasible) the bound $a_2(t)$ rises and falls so that the row activates at $t=1/6$ and deactivates at $t=5/6$. Along the sweep $\lambda_2\ge0$ throughout, the torque changes by at most $1.8\times10^{-2}$ per step of $3.3\times10^{-4}$ in $t$, and by $2.9\times10^{-3}$ across either switch. In the impressed view, where the designer prescribes the $F_i$, the task torque $\tau_x=\bar J^\top F$ jumps at a switch unless the forces are re-mapped through EQ(forcebasis), because $\bar J$ changes. Let $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal A'$ be the active sets before and after the switch, and $\lambda_{\mathcal A}=\Theta_{\mathcal A}^{-\top}F$ the stacked multipliers of the prescribed forces. At an activation $\tau_x=J_{\mathcal A'}^\top\lambda_{\mathcal A'}$, where $\lambda_{\mathcal A'}$ is $\lambda_{\mathcal A}$ with a zero inserted for the new row; by EQ(forcebasis) the re-mapped forces $F'=\Theta_{\mathcal A'}^\top\lambda_{\mathcal A'}$ keep the torque, and since $\bar J_{\mathcal A'}$ has full row rank they are the only forces on the new stack that do. At a deactivation of row $i$, $\tau_x=J_{\mathcal A'}^\top\lambda_{\mathcal A'}+J_i^\top\lambda_i$, where $\lambda_{\mathcal A'}$ is $\lambda_{\mathcal A}$ without $\lambda_i$; since $J_{\mathcal A}$ has full row rank, no force on the new stack keeps the torque unless $\lambda_i=0$, which the constraint view ensures and prescribed forces do not. In the first example, re-mapping at the activation keeps $F_1$ and $F_3$ (in general, forces above the switching level and on the last level keep their values) and gives the inequality row $F_2=\Theta_{32}^\top\lambda_3=-11.501$; holding $F_1$ and $F_3$ and giving the new row $F_2=0$, as a barrier force at its threshold would, changes the torque by $11.501\,\bar J_2^\top$. A rule that drops the row when its own decoupled force $F_i$ returns to zero does not ensure $\lambda_i=0$: by EQ(forcebasis) it leaves $\lambda_i=-\sum_{j>i}\Theta_{ji}^\top\lambda_j$, which is nonzero in general.

SECNUM A sign on the multiplier is not a sign on the decoupled force

The multiplier of an active inequality row is non-negative, EQ(unikkt). Its decoupled force is $F_i=\lambda_i+\sum_{j>i}\Theta_{ji}^\top\lambda_j$ by EQ(forcebasis), and the second term has no sign. In the example of Section SEC(sec-ineq-switch), over the whole active interval $\lambda_2$ ranges from $0$ to $31.06$ while $F_2$ stays between $-11.50$ and $-4.02$: the force that the hierarchy attributes to the inequality task points against the only direction in which the constraint can push. With the row oriented the other way ($J_2=+\partial x_2/\partial q$, a lower instead of an upper bound on $x_2$), $F_2$ ranges from $+11.50$ to $+18.98$ over the same range of $\lambda_2$; the multipliers of the other levels change with the orientation. The sign of $F_i$ is set by the lower levels’ multipliers through $\Theta$, not by the inequality. Together with Section SEC(sec-unilateral): the sign of a unilateral multiplier survives neither a change of basis nor a pairing with velocity.

SECNUM Conflicts and soft priorities

When the stack is infeasible, the lexicographic solution is no longer a single QP. Each level minimizes its own violation inside the optimal set of the levels above, and the sign condition $\lambda\ge0$ belongs to the level at which an inequality is decided. A weighted problem, all levels in one QP with weights $w_i$, replaces strict priority by soft priority, and strict priority is its limit as the weight ratios grow. In a fixed-seed example ($n=7$, a two-row inequality at level 1 in conflict with a seven-row posture task at level 2) the weighted solution approaches the lexicographic one like $0.1218/w$, with $w=w_1/w_2$ the weight ratio: the distance is $1.2\times10^{-3}$ at $w=10^2$ and $1.2\times10^{-6}$ at $w=10^5$, where the inequality is violated by $2.0\times10^{-6}$. Soft priorities within a level, as in weighted hierarchical QPs, are this weighted problem restricted to the tasks of one level: the block Gram–Schmidt picture applies between levels, while within a level the weights decide how a conflict is shared. With conflicts, continuity across active-set changes needs care; Mansard et al. (2009) analyse the irregularities that unilateral constraints create in task hierarchies and show how to insert them at any priority level. Our continuity checks cover the feasible case only.
