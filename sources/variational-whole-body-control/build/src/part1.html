SECNUM The Remaining Action and the Principle of Optimality

SECNUM The window problem

Consider a window of $N$ steps. The remaining action is the least action that has to be paid from the current point $q$:

$$ V_N(q)=\min_{q_1,\dots,q_N}\ \Big\{\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}L_d(q_j,q_{j+1})+W(q_N)\Big\}, \qquad q_0=q,\quad q_N\in\Omega, \tag{EQDEF(VN)} $$

over admissible paths. It is the optimal value function of MPC; in mechanics it is Hamilton’s principal function of the initial point plus the boundary term, minimized over the endpoint in $\Omega$ (Section SEC(sec-characteristics) makes this precise). The set of points from which EQ(VN) has a solution is the solvable set $\mathcal X_N$; we write $V_N(q)=+\infty$ outside it and assume that the minima are attained (for instance, $L_d$ and $W$ continuous and $\Omega$ compact). The receding-window scheme re-solves EQ(VN) from the current point and follows only the first segment $q\mapsto q^+=q_1$ of the extremal path (Fig. 1).

SECNUM The principle of optimality

Everything in Part I follows from one observation, Bellman’s principle of optimality: the tail of an optimal path is optimal for the problem that starts where the tail starts. Split the sum in EQ(VN) after the first step. For a fixed first point $q_1=q'$, the minimum over $q_2,\dots,q_N$ is the problem EQ(VN) with $N-1$ steps from $q'$, so

$$ V_N(q)=\min_{q'}\big\{L_d(q,q')+V_{N-1}(q')\big\},\qquad V_0(q)=\begin{cases}W(q),&q\in\Omega,\\ +\infty,&q\notin\Omega.\end{cases} \tag{EQDEF(bellman)} $$

The recursion builds the remaining action one step at a time, from the boundary term outwards; the terminal constraint is carried by the value $+\infty$ of $V_0$. Write it as $V_N=\mathcal B V_{N-1}$ with the Bellman operator

$$ (\mathcal BV)(q)=\min_{q'}\big\{L_d(q,q')+V(q')\big\}. \tag{EQDEF(T)} $$

The one property of $\mathcal B$ used below is immediate from EQ(T): $\mathcal B$ is monotone, i.e. $V_a\le V_b$ pointwise implies $\mathcal BV_a\le\mathcal BV_b$. A cheaper arrival can only make the departure cheaper. (The dynamic-programming literature often writes this operator $T$; here the letter $\mathcal T$ is the kinetic energy.)

SECNUM Terminal ingredients

The recursion starts from $V_0$, which is finite only on $\Omega$, so whether the scheme stabilizes depends on $W$ and $\Omega$. The classical requirement (Chen & Allgöwer, 1998; Mayne et al., 2000; Rawlings et al., 2017, Ch. 2; Grüne & Pannek, 2017, Ch. 5) is a reference flow $\varphi_f:\Omega\to\Omega$, $\varphi_f(0)=0$, with two properties:

Terminal ingredients.
  1. A1 (invariance). For all $q\in\Omega$ the step $(q,\varphi_f(q))$ is admissible and $\varphi_f(q)\in\Omega$.
  2. A2 (boundary-term descent). Along the reference flow, $W$ decreases at least as fast as the action is spent: $$W(\varphi_f(q))-W(q)\le -L_d\big(q,\varphi_f(q)\big)\qquad\forall q\in\Omega.\tag{EQDEF(terminal)}$$

In the language of the recursion the two conditions say one thing. For $q\in\Omega$ the reference step is a candidate in EQ(bellman) with $N=1$, so $V_1(q)\le L_d(q,\varphi_f(q))+W(\varphi_f(q))\le W(q)=V_0(q)$; for $q\notin\Omega$, $V_0(q)=+\infty$. Hence

$$ \text{A1 and A2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_1\le V_0\quad\text{everywhere.} \tag{EQDEF(V1V0)} $$

Conversely, if $V_1\le W$ on $\Omega$, then from each $q\in\Omega$ the minimizer of the one-step problem is an admissible successor in $\Omega$ for which EQ(terminal) holds, and it can serve as the reference flow. So EQ(terminal) says exactly that one step of the recursion does not raise the remaining action. It is a property of $W$, $\Omega$ and one step of $L_d$; it has to be verified before use, and it is the same for every window length.

The original argument of Keerthi & Gilbert (1988) used the terminal equality constraint $q_N=0$ instead of a boundary term: $\Omega=\{0\}$, $W=0$, $\varphi_f(0)=0$. A1 and A2 then ask only that the rest step $(0,0)$ be admissible with $L_d(0,0)=0$, and Sections SEC(sec-monotone)–SEC(sec-descent) apply unchanged (the candidate path is extended by a rest step). This $\Omega$ is excluded by the standing assumption that $\Omega$ contains a neighbourhood of $q=0$, on which the upper bound of Section SEC(sec-asymptotic) relies. With $\Omega=\{0\}$ that bound has to come from controllability instead: $V_N\le V_1$ by Section SEC(sec-monotone), and $V_1(q)=L_d(q,0)$ wherever the step $(q,0)$ is admissible, which for EQ(rect) gives $V_N(q)\le\big(\tfrac{m}{2h}+\tfrac{h\kappa}{2}\big)q^2$, the same bound that Section SEC(sec-asymptotic) obtains with $\mathcal V=\mathbb R$.

SECNUM Descent for Every Window Length

This section derives the stability of the scheme from the recursion EQ(bellman), the monotonicity of $\mathcal B$ and the one-step condition EQ(V1V0), for any $N\ge1$ and any discrete Lagrangian.

SECNUM Monotonicity in the window length

Apply the monotone operator $\mathcal B$ to EQ(V1V0): $V_2=\mathcal BV_1\le\mathcal BV_0=V_1$, and by induction

$$ V_N\le V_{N-1}\le\dots\le V_1\le V_0 . \tag{EQDEF(monotone)} $$

Two consequences follow at once. The solvable sets are nested, $\mathcal X_N\supseteq\mathcal X_{N-1}$, because a finite $V_{N-1}$ forces a finite $V_N$; and $V_N\le W$ on $\Omega$ for every $N$.

SECNUM The descent inequality

Let $q\in\mathcal X_N$ and let $q^+$ be the first point of an optimal path (uniqueness is not needed). The recursion EQ(bellman) holds with equality at its minimizer, and EQ(monotone) at $q^+$ gives $V_{N-1}(q^+)\ge V_N(q^+)$: $$V_N(q)=L_d(q,q^+)+V_{N-1}(q^+)\ \ge\ L_d(q,q^+)+V_N(q^+).$$ This is the descent inequality:

$$ V_N(q^+)-V_N(q)\ \le\ -L_d(q,q^+). \tag{EQDEF(descent)} $$

In words: after one step, the action still to be paid has dropped by at least the action just spent. The same line gives recursive feasibility: $V_{N-1}(q^+)<\infty$, so $q^+\in\mathcal X_{N-1}\subseteq\mathcal X_N$ and the scheme is defined again at the next step. Nothing beyond EQ(bellman), the monotonicity of $\mathcal B$ and EQ(terminal) was used, and $N$ is arbitrary. The classical proof unpacks EQ(monotone) into an explicit feasible candidate (Mayne et al., 2000, Sec. 3); written out, it also covers solvers that do not return the exact minimum.

The same proof with an explicit candidate: $V_N(q^+)-V_N(q)\le -L_d(q,q^+)$
  1. Optimal action at $q$. Let $(q_0,\dots,q_N)$, $q_0=q$, be an optimal path of EQ(VN). Then $V_N(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}L_d(q_j,q_{j+1})+W(q_N)$, and the scheme moves to $q^+=q_1$.
  2. A feasible candidate from $q^+$. Shift the path and extend it by one reference step: $(q_1,\dots,q_N,\tilde q)$ with $\tilde q=\varphi_f(q_N)$. Since $q_N\in\Omega$, A1 makes the extra step admissible and puts $\tilde q$ in $\Omega$.
  3. Bound the optimum by the candidate. $V_N(q^+)\le\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}L_d(q_j,q_{j+1})+L_d(q_N,\tilde q)+W(\tilde q)$.
  4. Use the boundary-term descent. By A2, $L_d(q_N,\tilde q)+W(\tilde q)\le W(q_N)$: the reference step cancels, and $V_N(q^+)\le\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}L_d(q_j,q_{j+1})+W(q_N)$.
  5. Subtract step 1: $V_N(q^+)-V_N(q)\le-L_d(q,q^+)$. $\blacksquare$

For $N=1$ the shifted path is empty, the candidate is the reference step alone, and step 4 reads $V_1(q^+)\le W(q^+)$. Steps 3–5 use only that the plan applied at the next step costs no more than the candidate. A solver that is warm-started with the candidate and returns it whenever it finds nothing better therefore keeps EQ(descent) for the cost it computes, whatever its optimality, as in suboptimal MPC; Section SEC(sec-robot-rh) uses this.

SECNUM From descent to asymptotic stability

Strict decrease alone is not enough. A Lyapunov function must also be sandwiched between two comparison functions, $\alpha_1(|q|)\le V_N(q)\le\alpha_2(|q|)$ (Rawlings et al., 2017, App. B). Write the positivity assumption of Section SEC(sec-steps) as A3: $L_d(q,q')\ge\alpha_L(|q|)$ on admissible pairs, with $W\ge0$, $W(0)=0$.

  • Lower bound. Every term of EQ(VN) is non-negative, so $V_N(q)\ge L_d(q,q_1)\ge\alpha_L(|q|)$.
  • Upper bound near the rest point. By EQ(monotone), $V_N\le W$ on $\Omega$. Take $\varepsilon>0$ with $\{|q|\le\varepsilon\}\subseteq\Omega$ and set $\alpha_W(\rho)=\sup_{|q|\le \rho}W(q)+\rho$, which is of class $\mathcal K$ because $W$ is continuous with $W(0)=0$.
  • Upper bound away from it. If $V_N\le\bar V$ on $\mathcal X_N$, then for $|q|\ge \varepsilon$, $V_N(q)\le \bar V\le (\bar V/\varepsilon)|q|$. Since $V_N(q)\ge\alpha_L(|q|)$, such a $\bar V$ exists only if $\mathcal X_N$ is bounded. When $L_d$ is bounded on bounded sets of admissible pairs, as the example EQ(rect) is, a bounded $\mathcal X_N$ is also enough. Each point $q_j$ of an admissible $N$-step path ending in $\Omega$ lies in $\mathcal X_{N-j}\subseteq\mathcal X_N$, because its tail from $q_j$ is an admissible $(N-j)$-step path into $\Omega$ and the solvable sets are nested (Section SEC(sec-monotone)). So $V_N\le N\sup L_d+\max_\Omega W$ on $\mathcal X_N$, with the supremum taken over admissible pairs in $\mathcal X_N$. Continuous $L_d$ and $W$ and a bounded $\Omega$ do not make $\mathcal X_N$ bounded, but bounded steps do: if $|q'-q|\le D$ on admissible pairs (a speed limit $|v|\le\bar v$ gives $D=h\bar v$), then $\mathcal X_N$ lies within distance $ND$ of $\Omega$. Without such a limit $\mathcal X_N$ need not be bounded. For EQ(rect) with $\mathcal V=\mathbb R$, every point reaches $\Omega$ in one step, so $\mathcal X_N=\mathbb R$, and on it $V_N(q)\ge\tfrac h2\kappa q^2$ is unbounded.

Hence $\alpha_2(\rho)=\max\{\alpha_W(\rho),\,\bar V \rho/\varepsilon\}$ is a class-$\mathcal K$ upper bound on all of $\mathcal X_N$, and with A3 the descent inequality EQ(descent) becomes

$$ V_N(q_{k+1})-V_N(q_k)\le-\alpha_L(|q_k|), \tag{EQDEF(strictdescent)} $$

which yields asymptotic stability of $q=0$ with region of attraction $\mathcal X_N$. When no such $\bar V$ exists, a global bound may still hold. For EQ(rect) with $\mathcal V=\mathbb R$, EQ(monotone) and the one-step candidate $q'=0\in\Omega$ give $V_N(q)\le V_1(q)\le L_d(q,0)+W(0)=\big(\tfrac m{2h}+\tfrac h2\kappa\big)q^2$ for every $q$. This is a class-$\mathcal K$ choice of $\alpha_2$ on all of $\mathcal X_N=\mathbb R$, so the region of attraction is all of $\mathbb R$. Otherwise, restrict the region of attraction to a sublevel set $\{V_N\le\bar V\}$ with $\bar V>0$. It is invariant by EQ(descent), and on it the upper bound holds with this $\bar V$.

SECNUM What the window length does: the one-step window

The certificate EQ(terminal) involves $W$, $\Omega$ and one step of $L_d$; it does not involve $N$. What a longer window does is read off EQ(monotone): it enlarges the solvable set, $\mathcal X_N\supseteq\mathcal X_{N-1}$; it lowers the remaining action, $V_N\le V_{N-1}$ (appending a reference step never increases the action); and it leaves the certificate unchanged. It moves the cut between the part of the remaining action that is computed (the window) and the part that is estimated (the tail $W$); Section SEC(sec-split) shows this cut with numbers.

The shortest window, $N=1$, has no lookahead. The scheme becomes a local rule: from $q$, jump to the admissible $q'\in\Omega$ that minimizes

$$ V_1(q)=\min_{q'}\ \big\{L_d(q,q')+W(q')\big\} \quad\text{s.t. }(q,q')\ \text{admissible},\ q'\in\Omega, \tag{EQDEF(onestep)} $$

which is EQ(bellman) with $N=1$. All the stability-relevant information now sits in $W$, which must satisfy EQ(terminal) on $\Omega$ and only there, because the constraint forces every successor into $\Omega$. Each step trades the descent of $W$ against the cost $L_d$ of moving, and nothing beyond the next point is examined (a “greedy” path). The solvable set is the one-step preimage of $\Omega$,

$$ \mathcal X_1=\{q:\ \exists\,q'\in\Omega,\ (q,q')\ \text{admissible}\}\ \supseteq\ \Omega , \tag{EQDEF(preimage)} $$

larger than $\Omega$ and, when the steps are limited (a speed limit, or the torque limits of Section SEC(sec-robot)), typically still small (with no limit on the steps it is all of $\mathbb R^n$, Section SEC(sec-asymptotic)); from points outside it no admissible one-step path exists, and the scheme is undefined there (Section SEC(sec-robot-results) has an example).
