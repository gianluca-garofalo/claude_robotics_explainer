Part IV · Synthesis and verification

SECNUM Synthesis

The receding-window results grew from one root, the principle of optimality of Section SEC(sec-bellman): its monotone recursion gave descent (Section SEC(sec-proof)), its limit the Hamilton–Jacobi equation and the momentum (Section SEC(sec-hj)), its characteristics the extremals (Section SEC(sec-extremals)), its fixed points exactness (Section SEC(sec-exactness)), and its phase-space form the momenta, the multipliers of the equations of motion (Section SEC(sec-second-order)). The whole-body results draw on two further ingredients that do not follow from it: Gauss’s principle with tasks imposed as constraints (Sections SEC(sec-gauss-task)–SEC(sec-unilateral)), in which no boundary term enters and whose multipliers are the task forces; and one block factorization of the stacked mobility (Section SEC(sec-ldl)), from which EQ(extJ)–EQ(power) and the attribution rules of Sections SEC(sec-ineq-hier) and SEC(sec-power) follow by linear algebra. Gauss’s weight ties both to the root: with a boundary term, Gauss’s problem is the infinitesimal-window rule EQ(gausstorque); with a constraint, it is operational-space control EQ(osc); and its metric $M^{-1}$ is the inner product of the factorization, which rewrites the stacked multipliers as decoupled forces EQ(forcebasis). Strict priority, finally, is lexicographic, not the argmin of one remaining action (Sections SEC(sec-leak), SEC(sec-lexi)), and the stability arguments built on the mechanical energy (Sections SEC(sec-strict), SEC(sec-robot-condition) and SEC(sec-equilibria)) are classical results, not consequences of the root. Six threads ran through the construction; this section collects them, then the instances of the second one, the multipliers met on the way and a single dictionary.

SECNUM Six threads

  1. Multipliers are physical quantities. The multipliers of the equations of motion are momenta (Sections SEC(sec-so-augmented), SEC(sec-so-ostrogradsky) and SEC(sec-so-discrete)); the multipliers of tasks are forces (Sections SEC(sec-gauss-task)–SEC(sec-gauss-dual)), those of contacts are contact wrenches (Section SEC(sec-gauss-dual); on a floating base, in the plant’s own Gauss principle, Section SEC(sec-outlook)), and in a hierarchy the decoupled task forces are the same multipliers written in another basis (Section SEC(sec-forces)). All of them are covectors: their values depend on the coordinates, their pairing with velocities, a power, does not (Sections SEC(sec-covectors), SEC(sec-invariance)).
  2. Argmin versus certificate. One minimization, of the stage cost plus the rate of change of the boundary term, yields a controller (its argmin) and a certificate (the sign of its minimum, equivalently a Hamilton–Jacobi inequality). They answer different questions, and a good controller can fail the certificate (Sections SEC(sec-stationary), SEC(sec-numeric), SEC(sec-certificate), SEC(sec-robot-condition)). A certificate belongs to the pair of Lagrangian and boundary term.
  3. Weak versus strict Lyapunov functions. A Lagrangian that penalizes position can only be certified by a strict Lyapunov function. The mechanical energy is weak and certifies only damping (Section SEC(sec-strict)); the composite energy of Slotine and Li is strict (Section SEC(sec-robot)); for a strict hierarchy no single energy-type certificate is available, and the analysis is cascaded (Sections SEC(sec-leak), SEC(sec-cascade)).
  4. Invariant versus attributed. The window length does not change the certificate, and, for a given torque, the priority order does not change the total power injected through the tasks. What they change is how a total is split: between the computed window and the estimated tail (Sections SEC(sec-onestep), SEC(sec-split), SEC(sec-parallel)), or among priority levels (Sections SEC(sec-forces), SEC(sec-power)).
  5. Two Lagrangians. The variational Lagrangian (the cost we choose) and the physical one (the robot) are different objects (Sections SEC(sec-so-augmented), SEC(sec-equilibria)). Controllers that act through impressed forces shape the physical one and keep the natural inertia; controllers that impose tasks as constraints in a variational principle shape the inertia (Sections SEC(sec-two-ways), SEC(sec-meet)).
  6. Inequalities: signs and switches. Inequality constraints keep every structure above: the descent proof, the multipliers, the Hamilton–Jacobi certificate (with the supremum taken over the admissible set) and the block Gram–Schmidt picture (for each active set), for a fixed contact mode without frictional sliding and a feasible stack (conflicts and contacts: Sections SEC(sec-soft) and SEC(sec-outlook)). They add two things. Multipliers acquire a sign, but the sign lives in one basis and at one level: it carries over neither to the decoupled forces of a hierarchy nor to the power (Sections SEC(sec-unilateral), SEC(sec-ineq-signs)). And active sets switch. In the constraint view, for a feasible stack, a switch moves the attribution of forces and power at and below the switching level, never the torque or the total power. In the impressed view, where the $F_i$ are prescribed, the torque, and with it the total power, jumps in general; re-mapping the forces through EQ(forcebasis) at the switch removes the jump at an activation, and at a deactivation only if the stacked multiplier of the leaving row is zero there (Sections SEC(sec-ineq-switch), SEC(sec-ineq-power)). An active constraint also changes which boundary term is exact (Sections SEC(sec-kkt), SEC(sec-speedlimit)).

In every row the controller is the minimizer of one bracket and the certificate is the value of that bracket at the minimizer. Whether the certificate holds is decided by how the Lagrangian and the boundary term fit together, never by the controller alone: the same PD law is certified with one spring and not with another, and the same clipped law is certified by one remaining action and not by another. The repairs are of two kinds: inflate $W$ (Sections SEC(sec-exactness), SEC(sec-robot-rh)), or shape the Lagrangian to match it (Section SEC(sec-robot-condition)); an inverse-optimal construction (Section SEC(sec-plant-energy)) does the second by design. The weak/strict thread is the case in which no Lagrangian that penalizes position can be matched at all: at rest, where $\dot q=0$, the rate of any $W$ is $\dot W=\nabla_vW\cdot f(z,u)=-\pi_v\cdot f(z,u)$, and for an energy-type $W$ the velocity momentum $\pi_v=-Mv$ vanishes there whatever the position error. A strict certificate EQ(cert2) therefore needs a velocity momentum that carries the position error, that is, a position–velocity cross term in $W$, as in Slotine and Li’s composite velocity (Sections SEC(sec-strict), SEC(sec-robot-rule)), and the Lagrangian must still be matched to it (Section SEC(sec-robot-condition)). A strict hierarchy adds a separate obstruction: its projected task forces are in general not the gradient of one potential, so not even a single $W$ of the form EQ(energyW) certifies the whole hierarchy (Section SEC(sec-leak)). The natural candidate there is a boundary term per level in the decoupled coordinates $\zeta_i$; since every $\zeta_i$ vanishes at rest, each level’s term would need its own cross term, and whether the per-level descent defects then add up to a certificate is open (Section SEC(sec-cascade)).

Symbols that clash across the literatures are fixed as follows. $L$ always denotes a Lagrangian, so the block $LDL^\top$ factor of a hierarchy is written $\Theta$ (with the pivots written $\bar\Lambda_i^{-1}$); $\Lambda$ always denotes a task inertia, so the gain of Slotine and Li is written $\Gamma$; $\mathcal T$ always denotes the kinetic energy, so the Bellman operator, often $T$ in dynamic programming, is written $\mathcal B$; a short time is a step $h$ or a window length $\vartheta$, so that $\delta$ always denotes a variation; decoupled velocities are $\zeta$, the minimum-principle costate is $\eta$, and $\lambda_{\max}(\cdot)$ is the largest eigenvalue. Gains are $K_P,K_D$ in joint space and $K_{P,x},K_{D,x}$ in task space; $c$ alone always scales a boundary term (the cosines of Section SEC(sec-robot-plant) are written $c_1$, $c_2$, $c_{12}$), and constraint functions are written $\chi\le0$. Multipliers of inequalities are $\mu$ in the optimal-control parts and $\lambda_{\mathcal I}$ in Gauss’s principle; the friction coefficient is $\mu_c$. Four marks carry two meanings, told apart by context: an overbar marks a bound or level in Parts I and II ($\bar v$, $\bar V$, $\bar W$) but an object of the decoupled basis in Part III ($\bar J$, $\bar\Lambda$, $\bar C$); a subscript on $V$ is a window length ($V_N$, $V_\vartheta$, $V_\infty$), but in $V_{\mathcal V}$ it names the admissible set, as in $H_{\mathcal V}$; $p_j$ is the momentum at node $j$, whereas Ostrogradsky’s $p_1,p_2$ are indexed by derivative order; and $a(z)$ of Section SEC(sec-robot) is a torque, whereas $a_x$, $a_{\mathcal E}$, $a_{\mathcal I}$ are accelerations. The calligraphic $\mathcal R_h$ of Section SEC(sec-exactness) is $\mathcal B$ written on quadratic weights.
