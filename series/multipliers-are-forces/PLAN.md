## Series 1: The Remaining Action (`remaining-action`)

**Story spine.** A controller that plans over a short window and applies only the first move converges because the
action still left to pay can only go down.
- Episode 1 makes that argument in discrete time.
- Episode 2 takes the continuous limit, where one minimization gives two things: a controller (its argmin) and a
  certificate (the sign of its minimum).
- Episode 3 makes it concrete on a hilltop solved by hand.
- Episodes 4 and 5 turn the certificate into practical warnings. A discretization can break it while the state still
  converges (episode 4), and a speed limit can leave the optimal controller uncertified (episode 5).
- The last two episodes take everything to a two-link arm under gravity. In episode 6 the rule turns out to be PD
  control with gravity compensation, certified only by a matched cost. In episode 7 the receding window runs on the
  arm and descends at every step, given a small enough step.

The thread is *argmin versus certificate*.

Plan sentence: "A controller that plans a short window ahead and applies only the first move converges because the
action still to be paid can only fall; this series finds what guarantees that fall, and why a perfectly good
controller can still fail to prove it."

## Series 2: Multipliers Are Forces (`multipliers-are-forces`)

**Story spine.** In whole-body control one torque serves many prioritized tasks, and the question "what force acts on
a task?" turns out to have no basis-free answer.
- Episode 1 shows that the multipliers of the equations of motion are momenta. This is exact in continuous time and
  holds to first order in the step in a solver.
- Episode 2 shows that the multipliers of a task in Gauss's principle are the operational-space force.
- Episode 3 places the two families of controllers, impressed and constraint forces, and shows what certifies each.
- With several tasks, the priority order is a change of basis for the forces (episode 4).
- Inequality tasks add signs that live in one basis only (episode 5).
- The finale finds what survives every choice of basis and order for a given torque, the total task power, and what
  that means for energy tanks. It closes on the parallel with the window length and on the open problem of
  hierarchical certificates.

The thread is *invariant versus attributed*.

Plan sentence: "In whole-body control the Lagrange multipliers are physical: momenta, then forces on tasks; this
series asks what the force on a task really is when one torque serves many prioritized tasks, and which quantities
survive the choice of priorities."

## What is left out, and why

- **Asymptotic-stability details** (comparison functions, the class-K sandwich): one sentence in series 1 episode 1.
- **The all-windows propagation argument** (the receding-window rate identity and monotonicity): stated with one
  picture in series 1 episode 2. The tanh formula of episode 3 shows it in closed form.
- **Jacobi fields, conjugate points, the principal-function sign table, viscosity solutions**: clauses only.
- **The discrete step with an active bound and the KKT reactions** (mu = 0.45, p' = -0.50; the terminal constraint as
  (1 + mu) W): out of series 1 episode 5, which would otherwise need the discrete momentum as a premise. The arm
  finale shows mu = 39.2 as a caption only.
- **Ostrogradsky's momenta and the Ostrogradsky instability**: at most one sentence in series 2 episode 1.
- **The transcription dependence of "torque equals momentum"**: beyond one sentence and one or two shares in series 2
  episode 1.
- **The stability-of-equilibria literature** (Lagrange-Dirichlet and its converses, Painleve-Wintner, gyroscopic and
  Kapitza stabilization) and **controlled Lagrangians**: left out. They tell a different story and have no numbers to
  show. The *two Lagrangians* point survives as one sentence in series 1 episode 3 and one card in series 2 episode 3.
- **Kawai et al.'s plant-energy terminal cost**: a sentence at most.
- **Whole-body control as the zero-window limit of whole-body MPC** (stiffness 1.36 versus 2.86 N/m): this would be a
  fourth idea in either candidate episode. See the open questions.
- **Series 2 episode 4 extras**: the kinetic-energy split, inertial versus dynamic decoupling, Coriolis couplings
  between levels, conflicts and singular pivots. Dynamic consistency and decoupled inertias appear as one visual.
- **Soft priorities and conflicts** (the 0.1218/w limit): one sentence in series 2 episode 5.
- **Fourier's inequality and the three levels of complementarity**: one clause each in series 2 episode 5.
- **Floating base and contacts** (friction cones, impacts, contact modes): outlook material without numbers.
- **The six-thread synthesis, the multiplier table, the dictionary**: these are the series themselves.
- **Arm extras**: the knife-edge design, the Pinocchio cross-check, the O(h) torque gap of the one-step window, the
  saturated certificate (about 5.9), and the metric projection with Gauss's weight.
- **The verification and references sections**: never on screen. Credits go in the description, by name.
